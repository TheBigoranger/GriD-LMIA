This page separates the polynomial representation from the certificate used
to prove a matrix sign. For a residual $F\preceq0$, set $S=-F$; for
$F\succeq0$, set $S=F$. The mathematical question is then

$$
S(\boldsymbol\alpha)\succeq0,
\qquad \boldsymbol\alpha\in[0,1]^\ell.
$$

Scalar formulas extend to polynomial matrices through matrix-SOS Gram
representations, or by applying the structured certificate to
$y^{\mathsf T}S(\boldsymbol\alpha)y$. Positive-semidefinite Gram matrices
certify non-strict inequalities. A strict margin must already be present in
the residual; the package does not insert one.

## A Tiny Example: Positive Polynomial, Failed Direct Test

Consider

$$
p(\alpha)=(\alpha-\tfrac12)^2+0.1
=\alpha^2-\alpha+0.35.
$$

It is strictly positive on `[0,1]`, with minimum `0.1`. Its degree-two
Bernstein representation is

$$
p(\alpha)=0.35B_0^2(\alpha)-0.15B_1^2(\alpha)+0.35B_2^2(\alpha).
$$

The negative middle coefficient means the direct Bernstein test cannot prove
positivity. It does **not** mean the polynomial is negative. A stronger box
certificate can use the identity

$$
p(\alpha)=(\alpha-\tfrac12)^2+0.1
$$

as a sum of squares with a positive constant. This one example explains why
the package separates the stored polynomial from the certificate selected for
it.

## Full-Space SOS

The simplest Gram certificate ignores the box and proves global
nonnegativity:

$$
p(z)=v_d(z)^{\mathsf T}Qv_d(z),
\qquad Q\succeq0,
$$

where $v_d$ contains monomials through a selected degree. Coefficient
matching is linear in $Q$. This is an SOS certificate on all of
$\mathbb R^\ell$, so it may be much stronger than positivity only on a box.
It is sufficient, but a multivariate nonnegative polynomial need not be SOS.
The package has no full-space SOS constructor.

## Direct Bernstein Coefficient Certificate

On a local box, write

$$
S_c(\boldsymbol\alpha)=
\sum_{\boldsymbol i}
B_{\boldsymbol i}^{m}(\boldsymbol\alpha)C^{(c)}[\boldsymbol i].
$$

Because the tensor Bernstein basis is nonnegative and sums to one,

$$
C^{(c)}[\boldsymbol i]\succeq0\quad\text{for every }\boldsymbol i
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
S_c(\boldsymbol\alpha)\succeq0
\quad\text{on the box}.
$$

This is the default [`pdlmi` constructor](/PD-LMI-package/documents/reference/pdlmi/constructor/)
certificate after residual and rate-vertex assembly. It introduces no Gram
matrices. The converse is false: an indefinite coefficient defeats this
certificate, not the continuous inequality.

## <span id="polya-type-coefficient-relaxation"></span>Pólya-Type Coefficient Relaxation

Pólya's theorem concerns a homogeneous polynomial strictly positive on a
simplex. After multiplication by a sufficiently high power of the sum of the
simplex coordinates, all coefficients become positive. For each box axis,
$(1-\alpha_s,\alpha_s)$ is a point on a one-simplex and its entries sum to
one. Therefore

$$
\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}
\bigl((1-\alpha_s)+\alpha_s\bigr)^r=1
$$

changes only the tensor Bernstein degree, not the residual. A higher degree
can expose coefficient positivity that was not visible at the original
degree.

[`applyPolya(r)`](/PD-LMI-package/documents/reference/pdlmi/applypolya/)
implements one selected elevation increment followed by the same direct
coefficient test. It does not search for an exponent, add a margin, or create
SOS multipliers. Failure at one increment is inconclusive. Quantitative Pólya
bounds also retain strict-positivity assumptions; see
[Powers and Reznick (2001)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-4049(00)00155-9).

## Putinar Quadratic Modules

For

$$
K=\{z:g_j(z)\ge0,\ j=1,\ldots,q\},
$$

the quadratic module generated by the constraints contains polynomials

$$
\sigma_0(z)+\sum_{j=1}^{q}\sigma_j(z)g_j(z),
\qquad \sigma_j\ \text{SOS}.
$$

Putinar's Positivstellensatz states that every polynomial strictly positive on
$K$ has such a representation when the quadratic module is Archimedean.
Compactness alone is not Putinar's hypothesis. Truncating multiplier degrees
produces finite SDP relaxations, but failure at one order is not a
nonnegativity counterexample. See
[Putinar (1993)](https://www.jstor.org/stable/24897130).

### Implemented Putinar box module

The package implements one fixed-order specialization on every local
hypercube. With the axis generators
$h_s=\alpha_s(1-\alpha_s)$, the sign-normalized target is represented as

$$
S(\boldsymbol\alpha)=S_0(\boldsymbol\alpha)
+\sum_{s=1}^{\ell}
\alpha_s(1-\alpha_s)S_s(\boldsymbol\alpha).
$$

For a target of stored degree $m$, the absolute order satisfies

$$
r\ge\left\lceil\frac m2\right\rceil.
$$

The no-argument method uses equality. At order $r$, the unweighted Gram term
uses tensor Bernstein basis degree $r\mathbf1$. The $s$th multiplier uses
$r\mathbf1-\mathbf e_s$; blocks with a negative degree component are omitted.
After multiplication by $h_s$, every term has tensor degree $2r\mathbf1$.
The stored target is elevated to that degree and matched exactly, coefficient
by coefficient.

[`applyPutinar`](/PD-LMI-package/documents/reference/pdlmi/applyputinar/)
creates independent dense PSD Gram blocks and equalities for every physical
cell and active rate row. It adds no strictness margin and does not call a
solver. This callable path is not a general-domain or general-generator
Putinar interface.

## Schmüdgen And Full Preorderings

The preordering generated by $g_1,\ldots,g_q$ also includes every
square-free product:

$$
p(z)=
\sum_{\nu\in\{0,1\}^{q}}
\sigma_\nu(z)
\prod_{j=1}^{q}g_j(z)^{\nu_j},
\qquad \sigma_\nu\ \text{SOS}.
$$

Schmüdgen's Positivstellensatz gives such a representation for polynomials
strictly positive on a compact basic closed semialgebraic set. Compared with a
quadratic module, the full preordering can require many more multiplier
blocks. See [Schmüdgen (1991)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01446568).

For the hypercube, the package uses the $\ell$ generators
$h_s=\alpha_s(1-\alpha_s)$. Their nonnegative set is exactly
$[0,1]^\ell$, and the $2^\ell$ subset products form the implemented
box-specific full preordering. At a selected absolute order,
[`applyFullBoxPreorder`](/PD-LMI-package/documents/reference/pdlmi/applyfullboxpreorder/)
creates independent Gram blocks for every physical cell and active rate row,
then matches the resulting Bernstein coefficients exactly. This is not a
general-domain Schmüdgen interface.

### Worked full-box example: two parameters, degree three

This example shows the finite SDP that the package assembles, rather than an
inequality that remains quantified over the box. Fix one physical cell and
one active rate row. Write its sign-normalized residual in local coordinates
$(a,b)\in[0,1]^2$ as

$$
F(a,b)=\sum_{i=0}^{3}\sum_{k=0}^{3}
B_i^3(a)B_k^3(b)F_{i,k}.
$$

Here $m=3$ and $\ell=2$, so the default full-box order is
$r=\lceil m/2\rceil=2$. The implementation first elevates $F$ exactly to
tensor degree $(4,4)$:

$$
F(a,b)=\sum_{p=0}^{4}\sum_{q=0}^{4}
B_p^4(a)B_q^4(b)\widetilde F_{p,q}.
$$

For example,
$\widetilde F_{0,0}=F_{0,0}$ and
$\widetilde F_{1,0}=\tfrac14F_{0,0}+\tfrac34F_{1,0}$. It then introduces
four independent positive-semidefinite Gram matrices and imposes the
identity

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(a,b)={}&Z_{2,2}^{\mathsf T}Q_{\varnothing}Z_{2,2}
 +a(1-a)Z_{1,2}^{\mathsf T}Q_{\{1\}}Z_{1,2}\\
&+b(1-b)Z_{2,1}^{\mathsf T}Q_{\{2\}}Z_{2,1}\\
&+a(1-a)b(1-b)
 Z_{1,1}^{\mathsf T}Q_{\{1,2\}}Z_{1,1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

$Z_{u,v}$ is the tensor Bernstein basis of degree $(u,v)$, with each scalar
basis function tensored with the identity of the residual-matrix size $n$.
Consequently, the four Gram matrices have dimensions

$$
9n\mathbin{\times}9n,\quad 6n\mathbin{\times}6n,\quad
6n\mathbin{\times}6n,\quad 4n\mathbin{\times}4n,
$$

respectively, and the finite LMI part is

$$
Q_{\varnothing}\succeq0,\qquad Q_{\{1\}}\succeq0,\qquad
Q_{\{2\}}\succeq0,\qquad Q_{\{1,2\}}\succeq0.
$$

The polynomial identity above is implemented as 25 exact matrix equalities,
one for each $(p,q)\in\{0,\ldots,4\}^2$. If $[Q]_{u,v}$ denotes the
$n\mathbin{\times}n$ block indexed by two tensor-basis labels, the first two
equalities make the mechanism concrete:

$$
F_{0,0}=[Q_{\varnothing}]_{(0,0),(0,0)},
$$

$$
\tfrac14F_{0,0}+\tfrac34F_{1,0}
=\tfrac12[Q_{\varnothing}]_{(0,0),(1,0)}
+\tfrac12[Q_{\varnothing}]_{(1,0),(0,0)}
+\tfrac14[Q_{\{1\}}]_{(0,0),(0,0)}.
$$

The $b(1-b)$-weighted blocks cannot contribute to the coefficient labelled
$(1,0)$, which is why they do not appear in the second equality. Thus this
one cell/rate-row certificate is **four PSD-LMI constraints plus 25 affine
matrix equalities**. It is an SDP formulation, not 25 further LMIs. Other
cells and active rate rows receive independent copies of exactly this system.
For a `<=` residual, the same construction matches the coefficients of
$-F$ instead.

## One-Dimensional Markov–Lukács Forms

On $[0,1]$, interval nonnegativity has exact parity-specific forms. For
degree at most $2d$,

$$
p(\alpha)=
v_d(\alpha)^{\mathsf T}Q_0v_d(\alpha)
+\alpha(1-\alpha)
v_{d-1}(\alpha)^{\mathsf T}Q_1v_{d-1}(\alpha),
\qquad Q_0,Q_1\succeq0.
$$

For degree at most $2d+1$,

$$
p(\alpha)=
(1-\alpha)v_d(\alpha)^{\mathsf T}Q_0v_d(\alpha)
+\alpha v_d(\alpha)^{\mathsf T}Q_1v_d(\alpha),
\qquad Q_0,Q_1\succeq0.
$$

These forms characterize interval nonnegativity at the stated parity rather
than supplying only one hierarchy level. The one-parameter full-box path uses
their Bernstein-basis matrix-Gram counterpart.

## Four Operations That Are Not Interchangeable

1. **Higher decision degree** enlarges the decision-function search space.
2. **Degree elevation** re-expresses one fixed residual exactly.
3. **Physical subdivision** re-expresses the residual on smaller cells.
4. **Higher Gram order** enlarges a positivity certificate.

Only the second operation is performed by `applyPolya`; the fourth is selected
at one explicit or default order by `applyPutinar` or
`applyFullBoxPreorder`. Their method pages place unsupported adaptive and
general-purpose boundaries beside the callable syntax.

## Exact Software Boundary

The callable package boundary is limited to:

- direct coefficient assembly;
- fixed-increment Pólya elevation followed by direct assembly;
- fixed-order Putinar assembly with the separate box generators
  $\alpha_s(1-\alpha_s)$; and
- fixed-order full box preordering, including the parity-specific
  one-dimensional form.

Full-space SOS, general-domain or general-generator Putinar modules,
general-domain preorderings, adaptive order search or automatic escalation,
adaptive subdivision, automatic strictness margins, and post-solve residual
certificates are mathematical background, not public package APIs. The
no-argument `applyPutinar()` call selects $\lceil m/2\rceil$. The distinct
`applyFullBoxPreorder()` default selects $\lfloor m/2\rfloor$ in one parameter
and $\lceil m/2\rceil$ in two or more parameters.
