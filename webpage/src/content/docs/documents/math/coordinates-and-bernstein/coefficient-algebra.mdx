The package performs polynomial algebra on each physical cell in the tensor Bernstein basis. Elevation and multiplication change coefficient representations, but they preserve the represented function. The numeric plans described below may be reused across compatible cells and rate rows.

## Sparse Tensor Elevation

Let $\mathbf m$ be the source degree and let $\mathbf M\ge\mathbf m$ be the target degree. For a source label $\mathbf i$ and a target label $\mathbf k$, define the sparse elevation operator by

$$
E_{\mathbf M\leftarrow\mathbf m}[\mathbf k,\mathbf i]
=\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}
\frac{\binom{m_s}{i_s}\binom{M_s-m_s}{k_s-i_s}}
{\binom{M_s}{k_s}}.
$$

The binomial factor is zero unless $i_s\le k_s\le i_s+M_s-m_s$ on every axis. Therefore only those source and target labels form stored entries. If $C[\mathbf i]$ denotes a scalar or matrix coefficient, then

$$
C^{\mathrm{elv}}[\mathbf k]
=\sum_{\mathbf i}E_{\mathbf M\leftarrow\mathbf m}[\mathbf k,\mathbf i]C[\mathbf i].
$$

The implementation packs the coefficient matrices into one row and applies the operator as a sparse matrix multiplication. With $q$ scalar entries per coefficient matrix, this has the form

$$
\mathbf c^{\mathrm{elv}}
=\mathbf c\left(E_{\mathbf M\leftarrow\mathbf m}^{\mathsf T}\otimes I_q\right).
$$

`elevRow` builds this operator for one source and target degree pair. `elevData` reuses the plan for every coefficient row with the same source degree, including repeated physical cells and rate rows. The public [`elevate`](/GriD-LMIA/documents/reference/pdbase/evaluation-and-elevation/#pdbase-elevate) method rebuilds the same dynamic class with the elevated data.

## Normalized Product Identity

For tensor Bernstein expansions $P$ of degree $\mathbf m$ and $Q$ of degree $\mathbf n$, the product degree is $\mathbf m+\mathbf n$. Its coefficient at label $\mathbf k$ is

$$
R[\mathbf k]
=\sum_{\mathbf i+\mathbf j=\mathbf k}
\left[
\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}
\frac{\binom{m_s}{i_s}\binom{n_s}{j_s}}
{\binom{m_s+n_s}{i_s+j_s}}
\right]
P[\mathbf i]Q[\mathbf j].
$$

Matrix order is preserved. The coefficient pair $P[\mathbf i]Q[\mathbf j]$ is never exchanged with $Q[\mathbf j]P[\mathbf i]$.

## Three Product Kernels

The numeric route first multiplies every coefficient by its tensor binomial scale, applies `conv` or `convn`, and divides by the output scale. For example, the degree-one rows $[4,2]$ and $[0.5,3]$ give

$$
[4,2]\star_{\mathrm B}[0.5,3]=[2,6.5,6].
$$

The middle coefficient is $\tfrac12(4\cdot3+2\cdot0.5)=6.5$. This route is used only when both packed tensors are numeric.

The known–affine route uses planned label pairs and contracts known coefficient blocks with affine YALMIP blocks. For

$$
A(\alpha)=A_0B_0^1(\alpha)+A_1B_1^1(\alpha).
$$

$$
x(\alpha)=x_0B_0^1(\alpha)+x_1B_1^1(\alpha).
$$

the product coefficients are

$$
p_0=A_0x_0,
$$

$$
p_1=\frac12(A_0x_1+A_1x_0),
$$

$$
p_2=A_1x_1.
$$

The known blocks multiply the affine blocks directly, so the result remains affine when the public multiplication rules allow it. The generic fallback uses the same planned pairs and weights, but it accumulates each compatible payload product in repository order.

YALMIP `sdpvar` data must not enter `convn` because MATLAB convolution expects numeric arrays and cannot preserve affine symbolic block structure. `prodVals` therefore dispatches numeric data to tensor convolution and keeps symbolic data on the planned contraction paths.

## Differentiation And Rate Rows

Along local axis $s$, define

$$
\Delta_sP[\mathbf i]=P[\mathbf i+\mathbf e_s]-P[\mathbf i].
$$

Then

$$
\frac{\partial P^{(\mathbf c)}}{\partial\rho_s}
=\frac{m_s}{h_s^{\mathbf c}}
\sum_{\mathbf i}\Delta_sP[\mathbf i]B_{\mathbf i}^{\mathbf m-\mathbf e_s}.
$$

Each nonzero partial is elevated to a common tensor degree before rate weighting. A zero-degree axis contributes zero. The resulting lower and upper rate combinations are stored as separate rate rows in deterministic tensor order.

## Equivalence Boundary

The optimized kernels preserve represented functions, existing YALMIP variables, certificate families, constraint order, PSD block dimensions, and cone partition, apart from ordinary floating-point summation differences. Numeric plan data may be reused. No execution-time comparison is reported. The optimization does not change an unsupported decision-by-decision product into a supported operation.

## Limitations

The algebra is cell-local on tensor-product boxes. Matrix products must remain dimensionally valid and affine in decision data. Function-only `pdmat` sources can be evaluated, but elevation and coefficient diagnostics require coefficient evidence.
