The package performs cell-local polynomial algebra directly in the tensor
Bernstein basis. The operations below are exact; conservatism enters only when
a finite positivity certificate is selected.

## Degree Convention

Use $\mathbf m$ for direction-wise decision degree and $\mathbf M$ for
direction-wise assembled residual degree. Products, known-data degrees,
derivatives, and componentwise common-degree alignment determine $\mathbf M$.
Pólya increment $\mathbf d$, absolute Gram order $\mathbf r$, and scalar
SparseFullBox bandwidth $w$ are certificate parameters, not polynomial degrees.

## Exact Degree Elevation

For a one-dimensional coefficient sequence $C[i]$ of degree $m$, one
elevation step gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tilde C[i]
&=\frac{i}{m+1}C[i-1]\\
&\quad+\left(1-\frac{i}{m+1}\right)C[i].
\end{aligned}
$$

with missing endpoint terms omitted. Tensor elevation applies this map axis by
axis. `pdbase.elevate()` and the protected `bernElev` backend preserve the
represented polynomial.

## Ordered Multiplication And Convolution

If

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\boldsymbol\alpha)
&=\sum_{\mathbf i}P[\mathbf i]B_{\mathbf i}^{\mathbf m},\\
Q(\boldsymbol\alpha)
&=\sum_{\mathbf j}Q[\mathbf j]B_{\mathbf j}^{\mathbf n}.
\end{aligned}
$$

then the product has degree $\mathbf m+\mathbf n$. Define

$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega_s(i_s,j_s)
&:=\frac{\binom{m_s}{i_s}\binom{n_s}{j_s}}
{\binom{m_s+n_s}{i_s+j_s}},\\
\Omega(\mathbf i,\mathbf j)
&:=\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}\omega_s(i_s,j_s).
\end{aligned}
$$

The coefficient is the ordered convolution

$$
\begin{aligned}
R[\mathbf k]
&=\sum_{\mathbf i+\mathbf j=\mathbf k}
\Omega(\mathbf i,\mathbf j)\\
&\qquad{}\cdot P[\mathbf i]Q[\mathbf j].
\end{aligned}
$$

Matrix-factor order is preserved: `P*Q` does not commute coefficient matrices.

Products are allowed when decision dependence remains affine, such as known
`pdmat` by `pdvar`. Decision-by-decision and decision-by-rate products are
rejected.

## Differentiation And Rate-Row Storage

Along local axis $s$,

$$
\Delta_sP[\mathbf i]
:=P[\mathbf i+\mathbf e_s]-P[\mathbf i].
$$

Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial P^{(\mathbf c)}}{\partial\rho_s}
&=\frac{m_s}{h_s^{\mathbf c}}
\sum_{\mathbf i}\Delta_sP[\mathbf i]\\
&\qquad{}\cdot B_{\mathbf i}^{\mathbf m-\mathbf e_s}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The implementation elevates every nonzero axis-wise partial exactly back to the
common vector degree $\mathbf m$ before rate weighting. A zero-degree axis
contributes the zero partial.

The resulting rate-vertex index belongs to stored rows. Once those rows are
stacked, write the sign-normalized target as

$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{(\mathbf c)}(\boldsymbol\alpha)
&=-\mathcal F^{(\mathbf c)}(\boldsymbol\alpha),\\
S^{(\mathbf c)}(\boldsymbol\alpha)
&=\sum_{\mathbf i}C^{(\mathbf c)}[\mathbf i]
B_{\mathbf i}^{M}(\boldsymbol\alpha),\\
C^{(\mathbf c)}&=-\mathcal C^{(\mathbf c)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

without carrying the row index through every certificate formula.

## Residual Assembly

Known terms, decisions, and derivative rows are aligned to common grids and
degrees before the original residual $\mathcal F$ is assembled. A comparison
orients $\mathcal F$ into $S$. Direct and all opt-in selectors enforce
their finite constraints against exactly the coefficients of this target.

## de Casteljau Evaluation And Subdivision

Repeated affine interpolation evaluates a Bernstein polynomial. Retaining the
left and right interpolation edges yields exact subdivision coefficients.
Subdivision and evaluation do not introduce approximation error into the
represented polynomial.

## Traversal And Storage

- `cells()` traverses one-based physical cell indices $\mathbf c$.
- `labels()` traverses zero-based local labels $\mathbf i$.
- `rateVertices()` exposes the stored lower/upper tensor rows when present.
- `bernsteinTable()` reports the coefficient provenance appropriate to the
  object class.

The nested order is part of the public storage contract. See
[pdbase storage and inspection](/PD-LMI-package/documents/reference/pdbase/storage-inspection/)
and the class-specific `bernsteinTable` pages.

## Limitations

Algebra is cell-local on tensor-product box grids. Ordered matrix multiplication
must remain dimensionally valid and affine in decision data. Function-backed
`pdmat` objects evaluate from their function and do not expose a coefficient
table. Increasing degree or subdividing a representation does not by itself
strengthen every selected certificate.
