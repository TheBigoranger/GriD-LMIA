The software state is `Putinar`. In one parameter its implementation uses the
parity-specific Markov–Lukács interval form within that selector. The Gram notation below uses
$Z_a=\vect b_a\otimes I_n$ for matrix size $n$, where $\vect b_a$ is the
degree-$\vect a=(a_1)$ Bernstein basis vector and every Gram matrix $Q$ satisfies
$Q\succeq0$.

## One-Parameter Even Degree

For assembled degree $\vect M=(M_1)$ with $M_1=2r_1$, where
$\vect r=(r_1)$, represent the target as

$$
\begin{aligned}
G_0(\alpha)&:=Z_r(\alpha)^{\mathsf T}Q_0Z_r(\alpha),\\
G_1(\alpha)&:=Z_{r_1-1}(\alpha)^{\mathsf T}
Q_1Z_{r_1-1}(\alpha).
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus

$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{(\vect c)}(\alpha)
&=-\mathcal F^{(\vect c)}(\alpha),\\
S^{(\vect c)}(\alpha)
&=G_0(\alpha)+\alpha(1-\alpha)G_1(\alpha).
\end{aligned}
$$

with $Q_0,Q_1\succeq0$. The basis-to-coefficient Gram map is affine, and
every resulting Bernstein coefficient is matched exactly to
$C^{(\vect c)}[i]$.

## One-Parameter Odd Degree

For $M_1=2r_1+1$, use endpoint weights:

$$
\begin{aligned}
G_j(\alpha)&:=Z_r(\alpha)^{\mathsf T}Q_jZ_r(\alpha),\\
S^{(\vect c)}(\alpha)
&=(1-\alpha)G_0(\alpha)+\alpha G_1(\alpha),\\
Q_0&\succeq0,\qquad Q_1\succeq0.
\end{aligned}
$$

The minimum absolute order is $\vect r=(r_1)$ with
$r_1=\lfloor M_1/2\rfloor$ in one parameter.

## Multivariate Putinar Module

For $\ell\ge2$, define
$g_s(\vect\alpha)=\alpha_s(1-\alpha_s)$. The implemented module is

$$
S^{(\vect c)}
=S_0+\sum_{s=1}^{\ell}g_sS_s,
$$

where each $S_s$ has a free positive-semidefinite Gram representation and the
sum matches every target coefficient exactly. The minimum absolute order is
$\vect r=\lceil\vect M/2\rceil$ componentwise.

Each cell, stored rate row, and column-major matrix entry receives independent
Gram blocks.
These are non-strict software constraints. The theoretical
$S^{(\vect c)}\succ0$ condition requires an explicit
$S^{(\vect c)}-\varepsilon I\succeq0$ margin in the modeled residual.

## Reusable Bernstein–Gram Map

Let $\vect b_{\vect r}$ collect the tensor Bernstein basis labels of degree $\vect r$, and set $Z_{\vect r}=\vect b_{\vect r}\otimes I_n$. For two basis labels $\vect a$ and $\vect b$, their product is

$$
B_{\vect a}^{\vect r}B_{\vect b}^{\vect r}
=\left[
\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}
\frac{\binom{r_s}{a_s}\binom{r_s}{b_s}}
{\binom{2r_s}{a_s+b_s}}
\right]
B_{\vect a+\vect b}^{2\vect r}.
$$

The bracketed factor is numeric. For a symmetric Gram matrix $Q$, a diagonal term $Q_{\vect a,\vect a}$ contributes once, while an off-diagonal pair contributes $Q_{\vect a,\vect b}+Q_{\vect b,\vect a}=2Q_{\vect a,\vect b}$. The coefficient at target label $\vect k$ is therefore the sum of all diagonal and paired off-diagonal contributions whose labels satisfy $\vect a+\vect b=\vect k$. Generator multiplication adds a second numeric Bernstein product map before the exact coefficient identity is stored.

For the small scalar basis $z=[B_0^1,B_1^1]^{\mathsf T}$ and $Q=Q^{\mathsf T}$,

$$
z^{\mathsf T}Qz
=Q_{00}B_0^2+Q_{01}B_1^2+Q_{11}B_2^2.
$$

The middle identity follows because $B_0^1B_1^1=\tfrac12B_1^2$ and the two symmetric off-diagonal entries contribute together.

`gramCons` builds the target-label groups, diagonal groups, paired off-diagonal
groups, generator maps, and tensor-window incidence once as fixed numeric plan
data. It then allocates fresh Gram variables for every matrix entry when the
inequality is entrywise, every physical cell, every rate row, and every
certificate block. Fresh Gram variables preserve certificate independence.

## Relation To FullBox

In one parameter, Putinar and FullBox use the same Markov–Lukács interval
form. In several parameters, Putinar includes only the unweighted and singleton
generator terms. FullBox includes every generator-subset product.

## Failure Interpretation

A fixed-order Gram model is a sufficient certificate. Failure may reflect
certificate order, numerical conditioning, memory pressure, grid choice, or
decision degree. It remains inconclusive about whether $S$ becomes indefinite somewhere.

## Limitations

The implementation is specialized to box generators and fixed absolute order.
Its supported scope is the box-generator module at a caller-selected order and
solver. Large Gram programs can be memory- or numerically
limited.
