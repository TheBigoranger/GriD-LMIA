Two distinct reductions occur in the workflow. Affinity in
$\boldsymbol\nu$ makes rate-box vertex reduction exact. Bernstein, Pólya, or
Gram conditions over $\boldsymbol\rho$ are finite sufficient certificates.

## Exact Rate-Box Vertex Reduction

For fixed $\boldsymbol\rho$, write the residual as

$$
\mathcal F(\boldsymbol\rho,\boldsymbol\nu)
=\mathcal F_0(\boldsymbol\rho)
+\sum_{s=1}^{\ell}\nu_s\mathcal F_s(\boldsymbol\rho).
$$

Because $\mathcal R$ is a box and the residual is affine in
$\boldsymbol\nu$, requiring the matrix inequality at every vertex of
$\mathcal R$ is equivalent to requiring it throughout $\mathcal R$. This
step introduces $2^\ell$ rate rows, ordered by lower/upper tensor choices.

The rate-vertex index is useful while explaining or inspecting these stored
rows. After rows are stacked into the finite residual array, certificate
formulas suppress it.

## Cellwise Derivatives

Within physical cell $\mathcal H_{\mathbf c}$, let
$\boldsymbol\rho=\phi_{\mathbf c}(\boldsymbol\alpha)$ and
$P^{(\mathbf c)}=P\circ\phi_{\mathbf c}$. Then

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial\rho_s}
(\phi_{\mathbf c}(\boldsymbol\alpha))
=\frac{1}{h_s^{\mathbf c}}
\frac{\partial P^{(\mathbf c)}}{\partial\alpha_s}
(\boldsymbol\alpha).
$$

`rhodiff(P, rateBounds)` differentiates cell-local Bernstein data, aligns the
partial derivatives to a common tensor degree, multiplies them by each rate
vertex, and stores the resulting rows. See
[`rhodiff`](/PD-LMI-package/documents/reference/pdvar/rhodiff/) for its input
and output contract.

## Shared Values, Different One-Sided Slopes

A continuous piecewise-polynomial `pdvar` shares full face-control handles
between neighboring cells. Therefore its values agree on each face. The
normal derivative need not agree:

$$
P_{\mathbf c^-}|_{\text{face}}
=P_{\mathbf c^+}|_{\text{face}},
\qquad
\nabla P_{\mathbf c^-}|_{\text{face}}
\ne
\nabla P_{\mathbf c^+}|_{\text{face}}
\quad\text{is allowed}.
$$

This is $C^0$ piecewise-polynomial storage, not a claim of global $C^1$
smoothness.

## Clarke Interface Coverage

A continuous piecewise-polynomial matrix function on compact
$\mathcal P$ is locally Lipschitz. At a shared face, its Clarke generalized
derivative lies in the convex hull of the limiting cell-side derivatives.
Because the DPD-LMI is affine in those derivatives, imposing the inequality
for every adjacent cell-side limit covers that convex hull.

This argument closes the interface gap without requiring derivative
continuity. It applies to the continuous decision $P$, not automatically to
discontinuous coefficient-backed `pdmat` data.

## Exact Versus Sufficient

| Step | Logical status | What remains |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Rate-box vertices | Exact for affine $\boldsymbol\nu$-dependence | Continuous $\boldsymbol\rho$-domain |
| Cell-side interface checks | Covers Clarke convex hull under continuity and affinity | Positivity on each cell |
| Direct, Pólya, or Gram certificate | Finite sufficient condition | Possible conservatism at fixed settings |

A failed certificate does not undo exact rate reduction, and it does not prove
that the original continuous inequality fails.

## Limitations

The exact vertex statement requires affine dependence on
$\boldsymbol\nu$ over a hyperrectangle. The interface argument requires
value continuity and local Lipschitz regularity of the decision function.
Discontinuous known data must be analyzed cellwise, and nonlinear rate
dependence would need a different treatment.
