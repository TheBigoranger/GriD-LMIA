## LPV Plant And Objective

Consider

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot x(t)
&=A(\boldsymbol\rho(t))x(t)\\
&\quad+B(\boldsymbol\rho(t))w(t),\\
z(t)
&=C(\boldsymbol\rho(t))x(t)\\
&\quad+D(\boldsymbol\rho(t))w(t).
\end{aligned}
$$

where $x(t)$ is the plant state, $w(t)$ is the disturbance, and $z(t)$
is the performance output. The scheduling vector and rate satisfy

$$
\boldsymbol\rho(t)\in\mathcal P,\qquad
\boldsymbol\nu(t)=\dot{\boldsymbol\rho}(t)\in\mathcal R,
$$

with parameter and rate boxes

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal P&=\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}
[\underline\rho_s,\overline\rho_s],\\
\mathcal R&=\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}
[\underline\nu_s,\overline\nu_s].
\end{aligned}
$$

The goal is a zero-state induced-$L_2$ bound
$\lVert z\rVert_2<\gamma\lVert w\rVert_2$.

## Bounded-Real DPD-LMI

Choose a differentiable symmetric matrix $P(\boldsymbol\rho)$. A standard
sufficient analysis condition is

$$
P(\boldsymbol\rho)\succ0
$$

Define the six independent blocks

$$
\begin{aligned}
L_{11}&:=\dot P+\operatorname{He}(PA),\\
L_{12}&:=PB,\\
L_{13}&:=C^{\mathsf T},\\
L_{22}&:=-\gamma I,\\
L_{23}&:=D^{\mathsf T},\\
L_{33}&:=-\gamma I.
\end{aligned}
$$

Then the second condition is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
\!L_{11} & L_{12} & L_{13}\!\\
\!L_{12}^{\mathsf T} & L_{22} & L_{23}\!\\
\!L_{13}^{\mathsf T} & L_{23}^{\mathsf T} & L_{33}\!
\end{bmatrix}\prec0
$$

for every
$(\boldsymbol\rho,\boldsymbol\nu)\in\mathcal P\times\mathcal R$, where

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot P(\boldsymbol\rho,\boldsymbol\nu)
&=\sum_{s=1}^{\ell}\nu_s
\frac{\partial P}{\partial\rho_s}(\boldsymbol\rho),\\
\operatorname{He}(X)&=X+X^{\mathsf T}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The strict signs describe the mathematical analysis condition. The package
inserts no hidden margin; a numerical model must encode a chosen
$\varepsilon I$ explicitly.

## General DPD-LMI Template

The same workflow applies to residuals affine in general decision functions
$y_k(\boldsymbol\rho)$ and their partial derivatives:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A_k(\boldsymbol\rho)
&:=F_k(\boldsymbol\rho)y_k(\boldsymbol\rho),\\
\mathcal F_{\mathrm{alg}}(\boldsymbol\rho)
&:=F_0(\boldsymbol\rho)
+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathcal A_k(\boldsymbol\rho),\\
\partial_s y_k(\boldsymbol\rho)
&:=\frac{\partial y_k}{\partial\rho_s}(\boldsymbol\rho),\\
\mathcal D_{ks}(\boldsymbol\rho,\boldsymbol\nu)
&:=R_{ks}(\boldsymbol\rho)
\partial_s y_k(\boldsymbol\rho)\nu_s,\\
\mathcal F_{\mathrm{rate}}(\boldsymbol\rho,\boldsymbol\nu)
&:=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{s=1}^{\ell}
\mathcal D_{ks}(\boldsymbol\rho,\boldsymbol\nu),\\
\mathcal F(\boldsymbol\rho,\boldsymbol\nu)
&=\mathcal F_{\mathrm{alg}}(\boldsymbol\rho)
+\mathcal F_{\mathrm{rate}}(\boldsymbol\rho,\boldsymbol\nu).
\end{aligned}
$$

The package supports affine decision expressions. Products nonlinear in
decision data, including decision-by-decision and decision-by-rate products,
are rejected.

## From Analysis Residual To Positive Target

A comparison such as `residual <= 0` stores the original cell-local residual
$\mathcal F^{(\mathbf c)}$ and normalizes its sign to a positive target

$$
S^{(\mathbf c)}=-\mathcal F^{(\mathbf c)}\succ0.
$$

The package assembles non-strict constraints. It does not silently replace
the original relation or add the explicit
$S^{(\mathbf c)}-\varepsilon I\succeq0$ margin required for strictness.

## Modeling Path In MATLAB

```matlab
yalmip('clear')

P = pdvar(n, grid, "symmetric", Degree=m);
Pdot = rhodiff(P, rateBounds);

residual = [ ...
    Pdot + P*A + A'*P, P*B, C'; ...
    B'*P,              -gamma*eye(nw), D'; ...
    C,                 D,              -gamma*eye(nz)];

L = residual <= -epsilon*eye(n + nw + nz);
selected = L;  % Direct by default

diagnostics = optimize(selected.toYalmip(), gamma.LocalValues{1}{1}, options);
assert(diagnostics.problem == 0, diagnostics.info)
Psol = value(P);
```

`pdlmi` assembles constraints but does not own solver selection or the
`optimize` call. Retain an optimized $\gamma$ only after
`diagnostics.problem == 0`.

## What Comes Next

The rate dependence above is affine, so the entire rate box reduces exactly to
its vertices. Parameter dependence remains continuous and requires a separate
finite sufficient certificate after gridding and Bernstein assembly.

## Limitations

This is an analysis condition for box-bounded scheduling parameters and rates.
It does not establish necessity, synthesize controllers, infer a grid, or
choose $\varepsilon$, a solver, or a certificate order. A nonzero solver
status is not proof that the continuous LPV problem is infeasible.
