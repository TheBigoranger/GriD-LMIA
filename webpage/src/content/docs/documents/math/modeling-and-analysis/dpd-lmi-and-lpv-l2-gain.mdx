pages carry the same problem through rate reduction, cell coordinates,

A <Term id="lmi" definition /> constrains an affine symmetric matrix expression. A <Term id="pd-lmi" definition /> lets its data and decisions vary with a scheduling parameter. A <Term id="dpd-lmi" definition /> also contains derivative and scheduling-rate terms. A <Term id="lpv" definition /> model provides a standard application for this structure.

## LPV Plant And Objective

Consider

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot x(t)
&=A(\vect\rho(t))x(t)+B(\vect\rho(t))w(t),\\
z(t)
&=C(\vect\rho(t))x(t)+D(\vect\rho(t))w(t).
\end{aligned}
$$

where $x(t)$ is the plant state, $w(t)$ is the disturbance, and $z(t)$
is the performance output. The scheduling vector and rate satisfy

$$
\vect\rho(t)\in\mathcal P,\qquad
\vect\nu(t)=\dot{\vect\rho}(t)\in\mathcal R,
$$

with parameter and rate boxes

$$
\mathcal P=\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}[\underline\rho_s,\overline\rho_s],
\qquad
\mathcal R=\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}[\underline\nu_s,\overline\nu_s].
$$

The goal is a zero-state induced-$L_2$ bound
$\lVert z\rVert_2<\gamma\lVert w\rVert_2$.

## Bounded-Real DPD-LMI

Choose a differentiable symmetric matrix $P(\vect\rho)$. A standard
sufficient analysis condition is

$$
P(\vect\rho)\succ0
$$

and

$$
\Biggl[\begin{matrix}
\dot P+\operatorname{He}(PA) & PB & C^{\mathsf T}\\
B^{\mathsf T}P & -\gamma I & D^{\mathsf T}\\
C & D & -\gamma I
\end{matrix}\Biggr]\prec0
$$

for every
$(\vect\rho,\vect\nu)\in\mathcal P\times\mathcal R$, where

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot P(\vect\rho,\vect\nu)
&=\sum_{s=1}^{\ell}\nu_s\\
&\quad{}\cdot\frac{\partial P}{\partial\rho_s}(\vect\rho),\\
\operatorname{He}(X)&=X+X^{\mathsf T}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The strict signs describe the mathematical analysis condition. The numerical
model encodes every chosen $\varepsilon I$ margin explicitly.

## General DPD-LMI Template

The same workflow applies to residuals affine in general decision functions
$y_k(\vect\rho)$ and their partial derivatives:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F(\vect\rho,\vect\nu)
&=F_0(\vect\rho)\\
&\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{N}F_k(\vect\rho)y_k(\vect\rho)\\
&\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{s=1}^{\ell}R_{ks}(\vect\rho)\\
&\qquad{}\cdot\frac{\partial y_k}{\partial\rho_s}(\vect\rho)\nu_s.
\end{aligned}
$$

The package supports affine decision expressions. Products nonlinear in
decision data, including decision-by-decision and decision-by-rate products,
are rejected.

## From Analysis Residual To Positive Target

A comparison such as `residual <= 0` stores the original cell-local residual
$\mathcal F^{(\vect c)}$ and normalizes its sign to a positive target

$$
S^{(\vect c)}=-\mathcal F^{(\vect c)}\succ0.
$$

The package assembles the original relation as non-strict constraints and adds
exactly the explicit
$S^{(\vect c)}-\varepsilon I\succeq0$ margin required for strictness.

## Modeling Path In MATLAB

```matlab
yalmip('clear')

P = pdvar(n, grid, "symmetric", Degree=mVector);
Pdot = rhodiff(P, rateBounds);

residual = [ ...
    Pdot + P*A + A'*P, P*B, C'; ...
    B'*P,              -gamma*eye(nw), D'; ...
    C,                 D,              -gamma*eye(nz)];

L = residual <= -epsilon*eye(n + nw + nz);
selected = L;  % Direct by default

diagnostics = optimize(selected.toYalmip(), gamma.LocalValues{1}{1}, options);
assert(diagnostics.problem == 0, diagnostics.info)
Psol = value(P);
```

`pdlmi` assembles constraints. The caller selects the solver and makes the
`optimize` call. Retain an optimized $\gamma$ only after
`diagnostics.problem == 0`.

## What Comes Next

The rate dependence above is affine, so the entire rate box reduces exactly to
its vertices. Parameter dependence remains continuous and requires a separate
finite sufficient certificate after gridding and Bernstein assembly.

## Limitations

This is a sufficient analysis condition for box-bounded scheduling parameters
and rates. The modeler supplies the grid, $\varepsilon$, solver, and certificate
order. Controller synthesis requires a separate formulation. A nonzero solver
status remains inconclusive about feasibility of the continuous LPV problem.
