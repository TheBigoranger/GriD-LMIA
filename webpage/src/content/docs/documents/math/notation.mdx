## <span id="domains"></span>Parameter And Rate Domains

$$
\mathcal P=\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}
[\underline\rho_s,\overline\rho_s],
\qquad
\mathcal R=\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}
[\underline\nu_s,\overline\nu_s].
$$

- $\boldsymbol\rho\in\mathcal P$ is the scheduling parameter and
  $\boldsymbol\nu=\dot{\boldsymbol\rho}\in\mathcal R$ is its admissible rate.
- $\ell$ is the number of parameter axes.
- $\mathcal V_{\mathcal R}$ is the set of the $2^\ell$ lower/upper rate
  vertices. Reduction to these vertices is exact when the residual is affine
  in $\boldsymbol\nu$.

## <span id="grid"></span>Tensor Grid And Physical Cells

$$
\mathcal G_s=\{\rho_s^{(1)},\ldots,\rho_s^{(k_s)}\},
\qquad
\mathcal G=\mathcal G_1\times\cdots\times\mathcal G_\ell.
$$

Here $k_s$ is the number of nodes on axis $s$. The one-based physical-cell
index $\mathbf c=(c_1,\ldots,c_\ell)$ selects

$$
\mathcal H_{\mathbf c}
=\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}
[\rho_s^{(c_s)},\rho_s^{(c_s+1)}],
\qquad
h_s^{\mathbf c}
=\rho_s^{(c_s+1)}-\rho_s^{(c_s)}.
$$

The tensor grid partitions $\mathcal P$ exactly. Gridding becomes a modeling
approximation only when it restricts the admissible decision-function space.

## <span id="local-coordinates"></span>Local Coordinates And Labels

The physical map $\phi_{\mathbf c}:[0,1]^\ell\to\mathcal H_{\mathbf c}$ and
its inverse are

$$
\rho_s=\rho_s^{(c_s)}+h_s^{\mathbf c}\alpha_s,
\qquad
\boldsymbol\alpha=\phi_{\mathbf c}^{-1}(\boldsymbol\rho).
$$

Physical nodes and $\mathbf c$ are one-based. A local Bernstein label
$\mathbf i=(i_1,\ldots,i_\ell)$ is zero-based.

<span id="bernstein-labels-and-degrees"></span>

## <span id="degrees"></span>Polynomial And Certificate Degrees

| Symbol | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| $\mathbf m$ | Direction-wise modeled Bernstein degree of a decision function. |
| $\mathbf M$ | Direction-wise assembled Bernstein degree of the residual or sign-normalized target. |
| $\mathbf d$ | Direction-wise Pólya degree-elevation increment. |
| $\mathbf r$ | Direction-wise absolute Gram order; it is not an increment from $\mathbf M$. |
| $w$ | Scalar SparseFullBox bandwidth. |

The public MATLAB properties store these degree and order vectors as
`1 × ell` rows. Scalar inputs are uniform shorthand. These quantities are
independent of the number of physical grid cells.

<span id="residual-and-positive-target"></span>

## <span id="residual-target"></span>Residual And Positive Target

On one physical cell,

$$
\mathcal F^{(\mathbf c)}(\boldsymbol\alpha)
=\sum_{\mathbf i\in\{0,\ldots,M\}^{\ell}}
B_{\mathbf i}^{M}(\boldsymbol\alpha)
\mathcal C^{(\mathbf c)}[\mathbf i],
$$

and a negative-semidefinite residual is sign-normalized as

$$
S^{(\mathbf c)}=-\mathcal F^{(\mathbf c)},
\qquad
C^{(\mathbf c)}=-\mathcal C^{(\mathbf c)}.
$$

The theoretical target is
$S^{(\mathbf c)}(\boldsymbol\alpha)\succ0$. YALMIP models non-strict
semidefinite constraints; encode a chosen margin explicitly as
$S^{(\mathbf c)}(\boldsymbol\alpha)-\varepsilon I\succeq0$.

## <span id="gram"></span>Gram Representation

For tensor Bernstein basis vector $\mathbf b_{\mathbf a}$, define

$$
Z_{\mathbf a}=\mathbf b_{\mathbf a}\otimes I_n,
\qquad
Z_{\mathbf a}^{\mathsf T}QZ_{\mathbf a},
\qquad Q\succeq0.
$$

The multi-index $\mathbf a$ specifies the basis degree, $I_n$ matches the
matrix-polynomial size, and $Q$ is a positive-semidefinite Gram matrix.
Weighted interval or box-generator terms change the coefficient-matching
degree without changing these symbol roles.
