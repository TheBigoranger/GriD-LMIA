## <span id="domains"></span>Parameter And Rate Domains

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal P&=\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}
[\underline\rho_s,\overline\rho_s],\\
\mathcal R&=\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}
[\underline\nu_s,\overline\nu_s].
\end{aligned}
$$

- $\vect\rho\in\mathcal P$ is the scheduling parameter and
  $\vect\nu=\dot{\vect\rho}\in\mathcal R$ is its admissible rate.
- $\ell$ is the number of parameter axes.
- $\mathcal V_{\mathcal R}$ is the set of distinct lower/upper rate vertices.
  Its size is $\prod_s(1+\mathbb 1_{\underline\nu_s<\overline\nu_s})$, which
  equals $2^\ell$ when every axis varies. Reduction to these vertices is exact
  when the residual is affine in $\vect\nu$.

## <span id="grid"></span>Tensor Grid And Physical Cells

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal G_s&=\{\rho_s^{(1)},\ldots,\rho_s^{(k_s)}\},\\
\mathcal G&=\mathcal G_1\times\cdots\times\mathcal G_\ell.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $k_s$ is the number of nodes on axis $s$. The one-based physical-cell
index $\vect c=(c_1,\ldots,c_\ell)$ selects

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal H_{\vect c}
&=\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}
[\rho_s^{(c_s)},\rho_s^{(c_s+1)}],\\
h_s^{\vect c}
&=\rho_s^{(c_s+1)}-\rho_s^{(c_s)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The tensor grid partitions $\mathcal P$ exactly. Gridding becomes a modeling
approximation only when it restricts the admissible decision-function space.

## <span id="local-coordinates"></span>Local Coordinates And Labels

The physical map $\phi_{\vect c}:[0,1]^\ell\to\mathcal H_{\vect c}$ and
its inverse are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_s&=\rho_s^{(c_s)}+h_s^{\vect c}\alpha_s,\\
\vect\alpha&=\phi_{\vect c}^{-1}(\vect\rho).
\end{aligned}
$$

Physical nodes and $\vect c$ are one-based. A local Bernstein label
$\vect i=(i_1,\ldots,i_\ell)$ is zero-based.

## <span id="degrees"></span>Polynomial And Certificate Degrees

| Symbol | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| $\vect m$ | Direction-wise modeled Bernstein degree of a decision function. |
| $\vect M$ | Direction-wise assembled Bernstein degree of the residual or sign-normalized target. |
| $\vect d$ | Direction-wise Pólya degree-elevation increment. |
| $\vect r$ | Direction-wise absolute Gram order, specified independently of $\vect M$. |
| $b$ | Scalar SparsePutinar tensor-window side stored by `CliqueSize`. |
| $w$ | Scalar SparseFullBox tensor-window side stored by `BandWidth`. |

The public MATLAB properties store these degree and order vectors as
$1\times\ell$ rows. Scalar inputs are uniform shorthand. These quantities are
independent of the number of physical grid cells.

The direction-wise label set, tensor basis, and coefficient count are

$$
\mathcal I_{\vect m}=\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}\{0,\ldots,m_s\}.
$$

$$
B_{\vect i}^{\vect m}(\vect\alpha)=\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}B_{i_s}^{m_s}(\alpha_s).
$$

$$
|\mathcal I_{\vect m}|=\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}(m_s+1).
$$

Even when $\ell=1$, the degree is written $\vect m=(m_1)$. Parity,
differentiation, and endpoint statements then refer to the component $m_1$.

## <span id="residual-target"></span>Residual And Positive Target

On one physical cell,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F^{(\vect c)}(\vect\alpha)
&=\sum_{\vect i\in\mathcal I_{\vect M}}
B_{\vect i}^{\vect M}(\vect\alpha)\\
&\qquad{}\cdot\mathcal C^{(\vect c)}[\vect i].
\end{aligned}
$$

and a negative-semidefinite residual is sign-normalized as

$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{(\vect c)}&=-\mathcal F^{(\vect c)},\\
C^{(\vect c)}&=-\mathcal C^{(\vect c)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The theoretical target is
$S^{(\vect c)}(\vect\alpha)\succ0$. YALMIP models non-strict
semidefinite constraints. encode a chosen margin explicitly as
$S^{(\vect c)}(\vect\alpha)-\varepsilon I\succeq0$.

## <span id="gram"></span>Gram Representation

For tensor Bernstein basis vector $\vect b_{\vect a}$, define

$$
\begin{aligned}
Z_{\vect a}&=\vect b_{\vect a}\otimes I_n,\\
G_{\vect a}&:=Z_{\vect a}^{\mathsf T}QZ_{\vect a},\\
Q&\succeq0.
\end{aligned}
$$

The multi-index $\vect a$ specifies the basis degree, $I_n$ matches the
matrix-polynomial size, and $Q$ is a positive-semidefinite Gram matrix.
Weighted interval or box-generator terms change the coefficient-matching
degree while preserving these symbol roles.
