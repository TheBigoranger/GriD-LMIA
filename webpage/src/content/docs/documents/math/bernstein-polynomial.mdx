## Begin With One Polynomial

On one local coordinate $\alpha\in[0,1]$, the modeled degree-$m$ Bernstein
basis is

$$
B_i^m(\alpha)=\binom{m}{i}\alpha^i(1-\alpha)^{m-i},
\qquad i=0,\ldots,m.
$$

After residual assembly, use $M$ for the target degree. For
$\mathcal F^{(\mathbf c)}
=\sum_i\mathcal C^{(\mathbf c)}[i]B_i^M$, define

$$
S^{(\mathbf c)}(\alpha)
=-\mathcal F^{(\mathbf c)}(\alpha)
=\sum_{i=0}^{M}C^{(\mathbf c)}[i]B_i^M(\alpha).
$$

Here $C^{(\mathbf c)}=-\mathcal C^{(\mathbf c)}$. The coefficients are
matrices, while the basis functions are nonnegative scalars that sum to one.

## From 1912 To Parameter-Dependent LMIs

Bernstein coefficients expose endpoint values and give a convex-hull
enclosure. For a sign-normalized positive target $S$, coefficient conditions
$C^{(\mathbf c)}[\mathbf i]\succeq0$ are therefore finite sufficient
conditions on a cell. They are not necessary in general. The theoretical
target is $S^{(\mathbf c)}\succ0$; software constraints are non-strict, so
model a chosen margin explicitly as
$S^{(\mathbf c)}-\varepsilon I\succeq0$.

## Local Coordinate, Basis Order, And Endpoint Labels

For physical cell $\mathcal H_{\mathbf c}$, use the physical map
$\boldsymbol\rho=\phi_{\mathbf c}(\boldsymbol\alpha)$ and local inverse
$\boldsymbol\alpha=\phi_{\mathbf c}^{-1}(\boldsymbol\rho)$. Local labels
$\mathbf i=(i_1,\ldots,i_\ell)$ are zero-based, even though physical grid
nodes $\rho_s^{(j)}$ and cell indices $\mathbf c$ are one-based.

The multivariate basis is

$$
B_{\mathbf i}^{M}(\boldsymbol\alpha)
=\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}B_{i_s}^{M}(\alpha_s).
$$

For scalar assembled degree $M$, each $i_s\in\{0,\ldots,M\}$.

## Nonnegativity, Unit Sum, And Convex Hulls

Because $B_{\mathbf i}^{M}\ge0$ and the basis sums to one,
$S^{(\mathbf c)}(\boldsymbol\alpha)$ is a convex combination of its
coefficient matrices. This is the mathematical reason the Direct certificate
is cheap and sufficient.

## Tensor Products On Hyperrectangles

The basis always lives on $[0,1]^\ell$; physical geometry is isolated in
$\phi_{\mathbf c}$ and cell widths $h_s^{\mathbf c}$. This separation
allows different physical cell sizes to share the same local coefficient
algebra.

## LocalValues, Continuity, And Boundaries

`LocalValues` is nested first by physical cell, then by local coefficient
label. A continuous `pdvar` shares complete face-control handles across
adjacent cells. This enforces equality of face values without separately
assembled continuity constraints.

Value continuity does not imply derivative continuity. Cellwise derivatives
may have different one-sided limits at a shared face; the DPD-LMI interface
argument is explained in [Rate-Box Reduction And Interface Analysis](/PD-LMI-package/documents/math/modeling-and-analysis/rate-box-and-interface-analysis/).

## Differentiation

Differentiation reduces the degree on the differentiated local axis, multiplies
by the degree, and scales by $1/h_s^{\mathbf c}$. `rhodiff` combines these
partials with rate-box vertices and stores one coefficient row per vertex.
Detailed formulas are in [Coefficient Algebra](/PD-LMI-package/documents/math/coordinates-and-bernstein/coefficient-algebra/#differentiation-and-rate-row-storage).

## Degree Elevation For Any Target Degree

Degree elevation is exact: it changes the Bernstein representation but not the
polynomial. It supports common-degree alignment and Pólya elevation. It is
different from increasing the decision degree $m$.

## de Casteljau Evaluation And Exact Subdivision

de Casteljau recursion evaluates a Bernstein polynomial stably and subdivides a
cell exactly. Subdivision changes the physical representation but not the
represented function.

## Product Degree And Coefficient Convolution

Pointwise multiplication becomes an ordered, binomial-weighted convolution of
coefficient matrices. Matrix-factor order is preserved. See
[Coefficient Algebra](/PD-LMI-package/documents/math/coordinates-and-bernstein/coefficient-algebra/#ordered-multiplication-and-convolution).

## Hypercube Counts And Traversal

There are $\prod_s(k_s-1)$ physical cells and, for scalar degree $M$,
$(M+1)^\ell$ local residual labels per cell. The public traversal and storage
contracts are documented under
[pdbase](/PD-LMI-package/documents/reference/pdbase/).

## Direct Matrix Certificates

After a comparison creates an original residual $\mathcal F$, `pdlmi`
normalizes its sign to a positive target $S$. Direct tests the matrices
$C^{(\mathbf c)}[\mathbf i]$ of $S$. Certificate formulas suppress the
rate-vertex index after stored rate rows have been stacked.

## Fixed-Order Putinar Box Certificate

Putinar uses free positive-semidefinite Gram blocks and exact coefficient
identities. In one parameter it uses the parity-specific Markov–Lukács
interval form; in several parameters it uses the unweighted term plus
singleton box-generator terms.

## Fixed-Order Full-Box Preordering

FullBox uses every square-free product of the box generators. SparseFullBox
keeps the same FullBox terms and exact identities while replacing each dense
Gram matrix $Q_J$ by a symmetric block-band Gram matrix $Q_J^{(w)}$ of
bandwidth $w$.
See the [certificate map](/PD-LMI-package/documents/math/sos-certificates/).

## Four Different Refinements

Decision degree $m$, residual degree $M$, Pólya increment $d$, absolute
Gram order $r$, and SparseFullBox bandwidth $w$ are distinct quantities.
Grid refinement is another independent choice.

## Control Literature And Scope

The package uses Bernstein structure to make a cellwise polynomial-matrix
inequality finite. It does not claim that one fixed grid, degree, or
certificate is exact for the original continuum problem.

## Limitations

The representation uses tensor-product box cells. Direct coefficient
positivity is sufficient, not necessary. Continuous `pdvar` values share
faces, but their gradients need not. Function-backed `pdmat` objects do not
expose a coefficient table, and discontinuous coefficient-backed known data
require cellwise interpretation.
