`pdvar` stores affine YALMIP expressions at cell-local Bernstein labels. The examples use deterministic metadata rather than unstable YALMIP variable names.

## <span id="pdvar-plus"></span><span id="pdvar-minus"></span><span id="pdvar-uplus"></span><span id="pdvar-uminus"></span>Build affine sums and differences

```matlab
R = P + Q; D = P - Q; U = +P; N = -P;
```

Compatible grids are aligned and direction-wise degrees are elevated to their
componentwise maximum before coefficient rows are combined. Numeric constants,
affine `sdpvar`, and coefficient-backed `pdmat` operands are promoted. `uplus`
preserves the expression; `uminus` negates every symbolic coefficient.

```matlab
yalmip('clear')
P = pdvar(1, {[0 1]}); Q = P - P; R = P + 2; S = -P;
Q.ContainsDecision
Q.Degree
size(R)
size(S)
size(+P)
```

```text
Q: decision=0, degree=0
R=1-by-1, S=1-by-1, uplus=1-by-1
```

The same exact degree alignment applies to symbolic rows and numeric rows. Use
the [`pdmat` numerical addition explorer](/PD-LMI-package/documents/reference/pdmat/algebra/#numerical-addition-explorer)
to inspect the coefficient transformation; `pdvar` replaces those numeric
entries with affine YALMIP expressions.

<Aside type="note" title="Implementation remark: exact addition alignment">
`plus` and `minus` use the same sorted-union grid refinement and componentwise
degree elevation as `pdmat`, then combine aligned affine coefficients. Shared
YALMIP decision handles are re-expressed rather than recreated, so the result
remains the exact sum or difference of the original decision expressions.
</Aside>

## <span id="pdvar-mtimes"></span>Multiply by known data

```matlab
L = A * P; R = P * A;
```

`mtimes` is allowed when decision dependence occurs on at most one converted operand. Numeric and coefficient-backed `pdmat` data may multiply a decision-bearing `pdvar` on a dimensionally valid side. An affine `sdpvar` also carries decision dependence after conversion.

For a quadratic decision and affine known factor on one cell,

$$
\begin{aligned}
(PA)[k]
&=\sum_{i+j=k}
\frac{\binom{2}{i}\binom{1}{j}}{\binom{3}{k}}\\
&\qquad{}\cdot P[i]A[j],\\
k&=0,1,2,3.
\end{aligned}
$$

Written matrix order is preserved; $P[i]A[j]$ is not exchanged with $A[j]P[i]$.
For tensor operands, the output degree is the componentwise sum of the two
degree rows.

The [`pdmat` numerical multiplication explorer](/PD-LMI-package/documents/reference/pdmat/algebra/#numerical-multiplication-explorer)
shows the same ordered Bernstein convolution with numeric coefficients.
For `pdvar`, the additional boundary is decision linearity: at most one
converted operand may carry decision dependence.

<Aside type="note" title="Implementation remark: ordered product convolution">
`mtimes` applies the planned normalized Bernstein convolution cell by cell,
adds the operand degrees componentwise, and preserves the written
matrix-factor order. The route is accepted only when decision dependence
occurs on at most one converted operand; two decision-bearing sides would
leave the package's affine YALMIP layer.
</Aside>

## Validation and operation boundaries

- Products with decision dependence on both sides are rejected as nonlinear.
- Products with rate dependence on both sides are rejected.
- Affine `sdpvar` multiplication requires the `pdvar` side to contain no decision dependence.
- Numeric and `pdmat` promotion still requires compatible physical bounds and matrix dimensions.
- Numeric or known data may multiply a derivative object while preserving its rate rows.
