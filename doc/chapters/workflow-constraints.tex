\section{From a Continuous Cell Condition to Direct}
\label{sec:cellwise-solver-path}
\index{residual}\index{Direct certificate}\index{finite certificate}

Fix any physical cell \(\mathcal H_{\vect{c}}\) and suppress the already enumerated rate-row index. After all local operations have been aligned to the assembled residual degree \(\vect{M}\), the pullback of the residual has the general form
\[
    \mathcal F^{(\vect{c})}(\vect{\alpha})
    =\sum_{\vect{i}\in\prod_s\{0,\ldots,M_s\}}
    B_{\vect{i}}^{\vect{M}}(\vect{\alpha})
    \mathcal C^{(\vect{c})}[\vect{i}]
    \prec0.
\]
Here \(\vect{\alpha}=\phi_{\vect{c}}^{-1}(\vect{\rho})\), \(B_{\vect{i}}^{\vect{M}}\) is the direction-wise degree-\(\vect{M}\) tensor-product Bernstein basis, \(\vect{i}\) is a zero-based local label, and \(\mathcal C^{(\vect{c})}[\vect{i}]\) is the corresponding residual coefficient. Define the sign-normalized positive target and its coefficients by
\[
    S^{(\vect{c})}(\vect{\alpha})
    =-\mathcal F^{(\vect{c})}(\vect{\alpha})
    =\sum_{\vect{i}\in\prod_s\{0,\ldots,M_s\}}
    B_{\vect{i}}^{\vect{M}}(\vect{\alpha})
    C^{(\vect{c})}[\vect{i}],
    \qquad
    C^{(\vect{c})}[\vect{i}]=-\mathcal C^{(\vect{c})}[\vect{i}].
\]
The theoretical target is \(S^{(\vect{c})}(\vect{\alpha})\succ0\) on the unit box. The Direct certificate imposes the finite non-strict family
\[
    C^{(\vect{c})}[\vect{i}]\succeq0
    \qquad\text{for every local Bernstein label }\vect{i}.
\]
Because every $B_{\vect{i}}^{\vect{M}}$ is nonnegative on the unit box and the basis functions sum to one,
\[
    \bigl[C^{(\vect{c})}[\vect{i}]\succeq0\ \text{for all }\vect{i}\bigr]
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    S^{(\vect{c})}(\vect{\alpha})\succeq0
    \quad\text{for all }\vect{\alpha}\in[0,1]^\ell.
\]
Repeating this implication for every physical cell and every stored rate row produces finitely many YALMIP constraints that suffice for the continuous sign condition. The implication is one-way because a positive matrix polynomial may have an indefinite Bernstein coefficient. Failure of Direct therefore leaves feasibility of the continuous condition inconclusive. MATLAB and YALMIP comparisons are non-strict. A strict theoretical target requires a user-selected margin such as \(S^{(\vect{c})}-\varepsilon I\succeq0\).

\subsection{Opt-in alternatives}

Direct is the default finite certificate. The package also provides opt-in \hyperref[sec:pdlmi-polya]{P\'olya}, \hyperref[sec:pdlmi-putinar]{Putinar}, \hyperref[sec:pdlmi-sparse-putinar]{SparsePutinar}, \hyperref[sec:pdlmi-sparse-full-box]{SparseFullBox}, and \hyperref[sec:pdlmi-full-box]{FullBox} alternatives. Their orders, Gram constructions, support rules, and validation boundaries belong to the detailed \code{pdlmi} chapter and Appendix~\ref{app:certificates}. Each alternative supplies a sufficient continuum certificate that is separate from parameter sampling and the solver call.

\section{The Complete Path to YALMIP}
\index{YALMIP}\index{solver status}\index{rhodiff@\texttt{rhodiff}}

The full workflow now fits in one panorama: represent known data, create continuous decision functions, differentiate them cell by cell when rate terms are present, assemble and compare a residual, select a finite certificate, export it, solve, validate the status, and only then recover numerical values.

        \node[dp/stage,right=of differentiate] (residual) {residual\\comparison};
        \node[dp/stage,right=of residual] (certificate) {finite\\certificate};

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
yalmip('clear')
grid = {[0 0.5 1]};
rateBounds = [-0.2 0.2];

a = pdmat(grid, @(rho) 1 + rho + rho.^2, ...
    Degree=2, RateBounds=rateBounds);
P = pdvar(1, grid, Degree=2, RateBounds=rateBounds);
dP = rhodiff(P);

residual = P - 0.1 * dP - a;
certificate = residual >= 1e-6;
F = certificate.toYalmip();
opts = sdpsettings('solver', 'mosek', 'verbose', 0);
sol = optimize(F, [], opts);
assert(sol.problem == 0, sol.info)
solvedP = value(P);
\end{lstlisting}

Here \code{rhodiff} evaluates the affine derivative term at the vertices of the stored rate box. That vertex reduction is exact. The comparison creates a Direct \code{pdlmi} by default, and an opt-in certificate selector could be inserted before \code{toYalmip}. Solver settings and objectives remain ordinary YALMIP choices. Recovery of \code{solvedP} requires \code{sol.problem == 0}. Any infeasible, numerical, unknown, or otherwise nonzero status requires diagnosis before decisions or objectives are retained. A failed sufficient certificate leaves feasibility of the original continuum condition inconclusive.
