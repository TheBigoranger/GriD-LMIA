This chapter develops one complete modeling spine: an LPV plant with an induced-\(L_2\)-gain objective, a bounded-real DPD-LMI, exact reduction of the scheduling-rate box, cell-local Bernstein representation on a tensor grid, a finite positivity certificate, and explicit YALMIP solution recovery. \section{Model an LPV Induced-\texorpdfstring{\(L_2\)}{L2}-Gain Problem}

Many systems are usefully linear only after their operating condition is made explicit. An LPV model retains the linear state-space structure while allowing its known matrices to depend on a measurable scheduling vector \(\boldsymbol\rho(t)=(\rho_1(t),\ldots,\rho_\ell(t))\) in a parameter box \(\mathcal P\). The canonical analysis model in this manual is
\begin{align}
\dot x(t)&=A(\boldsymbol\rho(t))x(t)+B_w(\boldsymbol\rho(t))w(t),\nonumber\\
z(t)&=C_z(\boldsymbol\rho(t))x(t)+D_{zw}(\boldsymbol\rho(t))w(t),
\label{eq:lpv-l2-model}
\end{align}
where \(x(t)\) is the plant state, \(w(t)\) is the disturbance, and \(z(t)\) is the performance output. The objective is to certify zero-state induced-\(L_2\) gain below \(\gamma\): \(\lVert z\rVert_2<\gamma\lVert w\rVert_2\) for every nonzero admissible disturbance and every admissible scheduling trajectory.

A differentiable parameter-dependent Lyapunov matrix \(P(\boldsymbol\rho)=P(\boldsymbol\rho)^{\mathsf T}\) yields the bounded-real DPD-LMI \cite{scherer1996}. Write \(\boldsymbol\nu=\dot{\boldsymbol\rho}\), let \(\mathcal R\) be the admissible rate box, and require
\begin{align}
P(\boldsymbol\rho) & \succ 0,
\label{eq:canonical-positive}\\
\mathcal F_{\mathrm{BR}}(\boldsymbol\rho,\boldsymbol\nu,\gamma)
&=
\begin{bmatrix}
\dot P(\boldsymbol\rho,\boldsymbol\nu)+\operatorname{He}\!\left(P(\boldsymbol\rho)A(\boldsymbol\rho)\right) &
P(\boldsymbol\rho)B_w(\boldsymbol\rho) &
C_z(\boldsymbol\rho)^{\mathsf T}\\
B_w(\boldsymbol\rho)^{\mathsf T}P(\boldsymbol\rho) &
-\gamma I &
D_{zw}(\boldsymbol\rho)^{\mathsf T}\\
C_z(\boldsymbol\rho) &
D_{zw}(\boldsymbol\rho) &
-\gamma I
\end{bmatrix}
\prec0
\label{eq:lpv-spine}
\end{align}
for every \((\boldsymbol\rho,\boldsymbol\nu)\in\mathcal P\times\mathcal R\). Here \(\operatorname{He}(X)=X+X^{\mathsf T}\) and \(\dot P=\sum_s\nu_s\,\partial P/\partial\rho_s\). The first inequality makes \(V(t)=x(t)^{\mathsf T}P(\boldsymbol\rho(t))x(t)\) positive definite; the second supplies the dissipation inequality for the induced-\(L_2\) bound. The strict signs shown here are analysis notation: the package inserts no hidden margin, so a numerical model must choose and encode conditions such as \(P(\boldsymbol\rho)\succeq\varepsilon I\) explicitly. Setting \(\boldsymbol\nu=0\) removes the scheduling derivative and recovers an ordinary PD-LMI.

\section{Follow the Package Path}

The package starts after the user has chosen an LPV model, a parameter box, any scheduling-rate bounds, and the matrix inequality to certify. Its role is to represent the known and decision matrices, convert the parameter continuum into finite certificate constraints, and hand those constraints to YALMIP. It does not generate the plant or controller structure, choose an objective or SDP solver, or interpret solver diagnostics on the user's behalf.

The main objects follow the same path used in the rest of the manual:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \code{pdmat} stores known parameter-dependent matrices, while \code{pdvar} creates continuous parameter-dependent decisions such as \(P(\boldsymbol\rho)\) and \(\gamma\)-dependent affine expressions. Both inherit grid, degree, label, cell-local storage, matrix-shape protocols, and shared structural operations from the backend class \code{pdbase}; ordinary users begin with \code{pdmat} and \code{pdvar}, not \code{pdbase}.
    \item Matrix operations assemble the original residual \(\mathcal F\). When scheduling-rate terms are present, \code{rhodiff} differentiates a \code{pdvar} on each cell and stores one derivative row for every vertex of \(\mathcal R\).
    \item A comparison such as \code{residual <= 0} creates a \code{pdlmi}, orients the comparison as a positive target \(S\), and selects Direct by default. \code{applyPolya}, \code{applyPutinar}, \code{applySparseFullBoxPreorder}, and \code{applyFullBoxPreorder} rebuild alternative finite certificates from the same original residual.
    \item \code{toYalmip} exports the stored constraints. The user calls YALMIP \code{optimize}, accepts an optimized \(\gamma\) only when \code{sol.problem == 0}, and calls \code{value} to recover numeric Bernstein data.
\end{enumerate}

Two finite steps in this path have different logical status. Traversing all vertices of \(\mathcal R\) is exact when \(\mathcal F\) is affine in \(\boldsymbol\nu\). By contrast, a Bernstein or Bernstein--Gram construction is a sufficient certificate over the remaining \(\boldsymbol\rho\)-continuum; failure at a fixed grid, degree, or certificate order does not prove that the original continuous inequality is infeasible. \Cref{sec:certificate-modeling-choices,sec:certificate-boundary} separates certificate selection from changes to the underlying model, and \cref{sec:cellwise-solver-path} executes the path in MATLAB.

\section{Version 1.1.2 Documentation Scope}
\label{sec:version-one-scope}

Version 1.1.2 is a documentation snapshot of the implemented workflow. The supported surface comprises the \code{pdbase} storage backend and its shared matrix-operation implementation; rate-aware \code{pdmat} known data and \code{rhodiff} derivatives; continuous arbitrary-degree \code{pdvar} decisions and their cellwise \code{rhodiff} derivatives; \code{pdlmi} Direct, P\'olya, Putinar, SparseFullBox, and FullBox assembly for decision expressions and coefficient-backed known inequalities; the documented MATLAB matrix-operation and indexing overloads; and the six public \code{helper} utilities. The source-indexed reference chapters state the accepted forms, returned shapes, validation rules, and error boundaries for that surface.

Centralizing shared matrix operations in \code{pdbase} does not remove those operations from \code{pdmat} or \code{pdvar}: both modeling classes retain complete public, class-specific reference entries and behavior. The package remains restricted to tensor-product box grids, affine decision expressions, and the finite certificate constructions described here. Products nonlinear in decision or rate-dependent data are rejected, certificate failure remains inconclusive for the original continuum inequality, and YALMIP retains ownership of objectives, solver selection, and diagnostics. Version 1.1.0 remains the latest tagged GitHub Release; v1.1.2 is a documentation milestone and creates no tag or release.

For release verification, run the root installer and \code{tests.run_all()} as described in \cref{sec:setup}. Examples involving \code{pdvar}, \code{rhodiff}, \code{pdlmi}, or solver state begin from a cleared YALMIP model so that their decision variables and constraints are reproducible in a fresh MATLAB session.

\section{Assembly Optimizations and Validation Diagnostics}
\label{sec:assembly-performance}

The v1.1.2 implementation substantially rewrites internal assembly while leaving the ordinary modeling path largely compatible. Bernstein elevation and multiplication build numeric operation-local plans once and reuse them across compatible cells and rate rows; Direct and P\'olya assembly preallocate in the established cell, rate-row, coefficient, and column-major entry order; Gram families reuse numeric coefficient maps while allocating fresh independent Gram variables for every required block; and \code{toYalmip} concatenates retained constraints once. These changes preserve the represented function, decision-variable identities, certificate family, constraint and cone order, and exact coefficient identities, up to scale-appropriate floating-point summation differences.

The public constructors \code{pdbase}, \code{pdmat}, \code{pdvar}, and \code{pdlmi} accept the transient name-value option \code{ValidationMode="fast"} or \code{ValidationMode="strict"}, case-insensitively; the default is \code{"fast"}. Each \code{applyPolya}, \code{applyPutinar}, \code{applySparseFullBoxPreorder}, and \code{applyFullBoxPreorder} call accepts the same trailing option. The selection is local to that call, is not stored as an object property, and is not inherited by later algebra or certificate-selection calls. The developer-facing raw \code{pdbase} constructor is a deliberate exception to broad fast-mode claims: it checks repeated coefficient structure only in the first supplied physical cell, so a later supplied cell can escape coefficient-count, payload, or rate-row validation; strict mode audits every supplied cell. The \code{pdmat} and \code{pdvar} constructors first normalize their public sources and retain their documented class-specific guarantees. In the normalized algebra and certificate paths, fast mode samples repeated post-normalization structural checks, while strict mode checks every generated cell, rate row, coefficient payload, elevation/product application, and Gram-plan application. Both modes retain option, compatibility, continuity, affine-safety, inequality-classification, and actual constraint-generation checks; strict is a diagnostic mode, not a different mathematical model.

\section{The Chain Rule in a DPD-LMI}
\index{chain rule}\index{parameter rate}\index{rate vertex}\index{rate row}

The dot on \(P\) is a time derivative along the scheduling trajectory, not a new independent matrix. Applying the multivariable chain rule gives
\[
    \dot P\bigl(\boldsymbol\rho(t)\bigr)
=\frac{dP}{dt} =\sum_{s=1}^{\ell}
    \frac{\partial P}{\partial\rho_s}
    \bigl(\boldsymbol\rho(t)\bigr)\nu_s(t).
\]
Give every scheduling direction independent finite rate bounds,
\[
    \mathcal R
=\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}[\underline\nu_s,\overline\nu_s].
\]
For a residual affine in \(\boldsymbol\nu\), the rate box is handled exactly by traversing its \(2^\ell\) lower/upper vertices \(\boldsymbol\nu^{(q)}\). The temporary index \(q\) is needed only while constructing the vertex blocks or explaining stored rate rows. After stacking those blocks, the rate index is suppressed in all parameter-domain certificate formulas. The coefficient-level differentiation and rate-row construction are derived in \cref{sec:bernstein-differentiation}. The rate-dependent inequality becomes
\begin{gather}
\label{eq:rate-vertex-block-diagonal}
\operatorname{diag}_{q=1}^{2^\ell}
    \mathcal F\bigl(\boldsymbol\rho,\boldsymbol\nu^{(q)}\bigr)
    \prec 0,
    \qquad \boldsymbol\rho\in\mathcal P.
\end{gather}

Each diagonal block is the residual at one rate vertex.  The remaining continuum is only the scheduling parameter \(\boldsymbol\rho\), which the package treats cellwise with its Bernstein certificates.

We therefore focus on the following general DPD-LMI.
\keyequation{General form of the differentiable parameter-dependent LMI}{%
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal F(\boldsymbol\rho,\boldsymbol\nu)
  = \sum_{k=1}^{N} R_k(\boldsymbol\rho)
  \sum_{j=1}^{\ell}
  \frac{\partial y_k}{\partial\rho_j}(\boldsymbol\rho)
  \nu_j
  + F_0(\boldsymbol\rho)
  + \sum_{k=1}^{N} F_k(\boldsymbol\rho)y_k(\boldsymbol\rho)
  \prec 0, \qquad \forall (\boldsymbol\rho,\boldsymbol\nu) \in\mathcal P\times\mathcal R.
\label{eq:general-dpdlmi}
\end{equation}
} Here \(R_k\), \(F_0\), and \(F_k\) are known matrix functions, while \(y_k(\boldsymbol\rho)\) are general parameter-dependent decision functions; \(P(\boldsymbol\rho)\) in \cref{eq:lpv-spine} is the canonical matrix-valued instance. Affine dependence on \(\boldsymbol\nu\) reduces exactly to \cref{eq:rate-vertex-block-diagonal}, but \(\boldsymbol\rho\in\mathcal P\) remains a continuum. The package represents each \(y_k\) as a continuous piecewise polynomial with cell-local tensor-product Bernstein coefficients.

Setting \(R_k\equiv0\) for every \(k\) removes the derivative term and recovers an ordinary PD-LMI.

\section{Grid the Scheduling-Parameter Space}
\index{physical grid}\index{physical node}\index{physical hypercube}\index{tensor grid}

The package currently considers only an axis-aligned box parameter domain,
\[
    \mathcal P=\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}[\underline\rho_s,\overline\rho_s].
\]
This restriction is deliberate, as a compact box is easy to partition into hypercubes for cellwise modelling. General polytopic or curved parameter domains are outside the implemented model. Gridding starts independently in each parameter direction. For each direction, choose the following physical grid
\[
    \mathcal G_s=
    \{\underline{\rho}_s=\rho_s^{(1)},\ldots,\rho_s^{(k_s)}=\overline{\rho}_s\},\qquad s=1,\ldots,\ell.
\]
The full tensor grid is \(\mathcal G=\mathcal G_1\times\cdots\times\mathcal G_\ell\).  It contains \(\prod_{s=1}^{\ell} k_s\) physical nodes and partitions \(\mathcal P\) into \(\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}(k_s-1)\) physical hypercubes.  A hypercube is identified by the  $\ell$-dimensional multi-index \(\mathbf c=(c_1,\ldots,c_\ell)\); \cref{sec:bernstein-tensor-grids} gives the exact physical-grid and local-label conventions:
\begin{gather}
    \label{eq:physical-hypercube}
    \mathcal H_{\mathbf c}=\prod_{s=1}^{\ell} [\rho_s^{(c_s)},\rho_s^{(c_s+1)}].
\end{gather}

\section{Desired Behavior Across Cell Boundaries}
\label{sec:desired-cross-cell-behaviour}
\index{shared face}\index{continuity}\index{Lipschitz regularity}

The package represents coefficient-backed known data and decision matrices by local polynomials, so products, degree elevation, and differentiation can be performed one physical cell at a time. A Lyapunov matrix or comparable decision function must not jump when the scheduling trajectory crosses a shared face, so \code{pdvar} guarantees global \(C^0\) continuity. It does not require \(C^1\) continuity, that is, one-sided derivatives may differ, and \code{rhodiff} retains the corresponding cell-local derivative data\index{derivative data}. Known \code{pdmat} data are different: the object records whether adjacent numeric faces match, but it preserves explicitly supplied discontinuous local data rather than adding decision constraints to repair them.

A monomial parameterization makes the additional continuity condition explicit.  For two adjacent one-dimensional cells, write
\[
    A^{(c)}(\alpha)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\alpha^i A_i^{(c)},
    \qquad \alpha\in[0,1].
\]
At their shared physical node, \(\alpha_1=1\) in the left cell and \(\alpha_2=0\) in the right cell.  Enforcing \(C^0\) continuity therefore requires the extra matrix equality
\[
    \underbrace{\sum_{i=0}^{m}A_i^{(1)}}_{A^{(1)}(1)}
    =\underbrace{A_0^{(2)}}_{A^{(2)}(0)}.
\]
When \(\ell>1\), equality along a shared face is a polynomial identity in the tangential coordinates and produces a corresponding family of linear coefficient equalities. The number of such conditions grows with the polynomial degree and the number of shared faces.

The right side of \cref{fig:shared-face-lipschitz} shows the intended behavior: the two polynomial pieces share their boundary value, while their one-sided slopes may differ. A globally \(C^0\), piecewise-polynomial matrix function on compact \(\mathcal P\) is locally Lipschitz. At a shared face, its Clarke generalized derivative is the convex hull of the limiting derivatives from the adjacent cells \cite{clarke1990}. Because \cref{eq:general-dpdlmi} is affine in those derivatives, imposing the cell-side conditions covers that convex hull; this interface argument does not claim that \(\nabla P\) is continuous. Discontinuous coefficient-backed \code{pdmat} data remain a supported implementation choice, but they fall outside this continuity argument and must be analyzed cellwise.

\section{Bernstein Basis and Cell-Local Storage}
\label{sec:continuous-bernstein-representation}
\index{cell-local storage}\index{local Bernstein label}

For its cell-local polynomial representation, the package uses the Bernstein basis rather than the monomial basis in the preceding comparison. Bernstein endpoint identities expose each face polynomial directly through its face coefficients; shared decision storage can therefore impose \(C^0\) continuity without adding separate cross-cell equalities. Inside \(\mathcal H_{\mathbf c}\), define its width and forward pullback by
\[
    h_s^{(\mathbf c)}=\rho_s^{(c_s+1)}-\rho_s^{(c_s)},
    \qquad
    \phi_{\mathbf c}(\boldsymbol\rho)=\boldsymbol\alpha,
    \qquad
    \alpha_s=\frac{\rho_s-\rho_s^{(c_s)}}{h_s^{(\mathbf c)}}\in[0,1].
\]
On one local coordinate \(\alpha\in[0,1]\), the normalized degree-\(m\) Bernstein basis is
\[
    B_i^m(\alpha)=\binom mi(1-\alpha)^{m-i}\alpha^i,
    \qquad i=0,\ldots,m.
\]
The basis functions are nonnegative and sum to one.  The first four nonnegative degrees are
\[
\begin{aligned}
    m=0:\quad
    &B_0^0=1                 &
    &\\
    m=1:\quad
      &B_0^1=1-\alpha,                 &
      &B_1^1=\alpha,\\
    m=2:\quad
      &B_0^2=(1-\alpha)^2,             &
      &B_1^2=2(1-\alpha)\alpha,        &
      &B_2^2=\alpha^2,\\
    m=3:\quad
      &B_0^3=(1-\alpha)^3,             &
      &B_1^3=3(1-\alpha)^2\alpha,      &
      &B_2^3=3(1-\alpha)\alpha^2,      &
      &B_3^3=\alpha^3.
\end{aligned}
\]
In \(\ell\) parameter directions, the package uses the tensor-product expansion
\[
    C^{(\mathbf c)}\!\left(\phi_{\mathbf c}(\boldsymbol\rho)\right)=
    \sum_{\mathbf i\in\{0,\ldots,m\}^{\ell}}
    \left(\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}B_{i_s}^{m}(\alpha_s)\right)
    C^{(\mathbf c)}[\mathbf i].
\]
The matrix value is a convex combination of the local coefficient matrices. Exact degree elevation, multiplication, and differentiation operate on those coefficients without changing the identity of the physical cell. The detailed formulas appear in \cref{sec:bernstein-higher-degree,sec:bernstein-degree-elevation,sec:bernstein-product-math,sec:bernstein-differentiation}.

\subsection{Physical nodes, local labels, and continuity by storage}

The two index systems have different meanings:
\begin{itemize}
    \item the physical node \(\rho_s^{(j)}\) and the cell component \(c_s\) are one-based and identify geometry; \item the local Bernstein label \(i_s\in\{0,\ldots,m\}\) is zero-based and selects one coefficient inside an already chosen cell.
\end{itemize}
A degree-\(m\) tensor polynomial stores \((m+1)^\ell\) local coefficient positions per physical cell.  These positions are not another grid of point samples.  The traversal order used by \code{cells}, \code{lbls}, \code{coeffs}, and rate-vertex rows varies later dimensions fastest; in two dimensions, \code{lbls()} lists \([0,0],[0,1],\ldots,[0,m],[1,0],\ldots,[m,m]\). The general tensor ordering is defined in \cref{sec:bernstein-tensor-grids}.

The endpoint identities \(B_0^m(0)=1\) and \(B_m^m(1)=1\) make continuity easy to encode in coefficient data. For a \code{pdvar}, adjacent cells reuse the same YALMIP decision handles on their common face; in one dimension, \(C^{(1)}[m]\) and \(C^{(2)}[0]\) are the same decision matrix. Tensor cells similarly share every handle on a common face, so continuity requires no later equality constraint. For a coefficient-backed \code{pdmat}, \code{IsContinuous} instead reports whether the two numeric face-coefficient sets agree; explicitly mismatched local data remain cell-local and produce a warning.

The first two-dimensional example uses \(\mathcal G_1=\{0,0.5,1\}\), \(\mathcal G_2=\{0,1\}\), and degree two:

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
grid2 = {[0 .5 1], [0 1]};
A2 = pdmat(grid2, @(rho1,rho2) ...
    [1+rho1+rho2, rho1*rho2; ...
        rho1*rho2,   2+rho1^2], Degree=2);
physicalCells = A2.cells()
localLabels = A2.lbls()
secondCellCoefficients = A2.coeffs([2 1])
sameLeaf = A2.LocalValues{2}{1}
\end{lstlisting}

The grid has two physical hypercubes. The highlighted one below is \(\mathbf c=(2,1)\), whose domain is \([0.5,1]\times[0,1]\). Selecting it through \code{A2.coeffs([2 1])} reaches the same nested storage leaf as \code{A2.LocalValues{2}{1}}.
