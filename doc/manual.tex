\begin{abstract}
This manual describes the current public behavior of the \pkg.  The package
uses \code{pdbase} as the backend parent for cell-local Bernstein coefficient
storage, provides \code{pdmat} for known finite real matrix functions on tensor
grids, provides \code{pdvar} for YALMIP-backed Bernstein decision expressions
and \code{rhodiff} derivatives, and provides \code{pdlmi} for direct
coefficient-wise assembly, opt-in P\'olya elevation, and an opt-in cell-local
full box Bernstein--Gram preordering.  The manual is written as a reference
manual: it begins with the canonical differentiable PDLMI and gridding model,
then separates setup, public-class references, shared helpers, examples, and
the mathematical Bernstein and positivity-certificate appendices.
\end{abstract}

\section{Canonical Differentiable PDLMI}
\label{sec:canonical-pdlmi}

\subsection{Problem Form}

Let the scheduling vector be
\(\rho=(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_\ell)\in\mathcal P\), with rate
\(\dot\rho\in\mathcal V\), where both sets are boxes.  A differentiable
parameter-dependent LMI asks for matrix functions such as
\(P(\rho)=P(\rho)^{\mathsf T}\) and possibly additional scalar or matrix
decisions such that, for every admissible \((\rho,\dot\rho)\),
\begin{align}
  P(\rho)&\succeq 0, \label{eq:canonical-positive}\\
  \mathcal F\!\left(\rho,\dot\rho,P(\rho),
    \frac{\partial P}{\partial\rho_1},\ldots,
    \frac{\partial P}{\partial\rho_\ell}\right)&\preceq 0.
  \label{eq:canonical-dplmi}
\end{align}
The residual \(\mathcal F\) must be affine in the optimization variables after
the known parameter-dependent matrices have been fixed.  A common Lyapunov
instance is
\[
  \sum_{s=1}^{\ell}\dot\rho_s
      \frac{\partial P}{\partial\rho_s}
  +P(\rho)A(\rho)+A(\rho)^{\mathsf T}P(\rho)\preceq0.
\]
The package does not discretize sample values and hope that the inequalities
hold between them.  It represents each cell residual exactly in a local
Bernstein basis, enumerates the vertices of the rate box when \(\dot\rho\)
enters affinely, and then applies a finite sufficient matrix-sign certificate.
Strict inequalities are modeled explicitly by the user, for example
\(P\succeq\varepsilon I\); the package inserts no hidden margin.

\subsection{Gridding One, Two, And \texorpdfstring{\(\ell\)}{ell} Parameters}

For one scheduling parameter, choose strictly increasing nodes
\[
  \rho_{1,0}<\rho_{1,1}<\cdots<\rho_{1,N_1-1}.
\]
Cell \(c_1\in\{1,\ldots,N_1-1\}\) is the interval
\([\rho_{1,c_1-1},\rho_{1,c_1}]\).  Its forward local coordinate is
\[
  \alpha_1=\frac{\rho_1-\rho_{1,c_1-1}}
                  {\rho_{1,c_1}-\rho_{1,c_1-1}}\in[0,1].
\]
Thus local label zero belongs to the lower endpoint and label \(m\) to the
upper endpoint.

For two parameters, two node vectors form rectangular cells indexed by
\((c_1,c_2)\).  Each cell is mapped to \([0,1]^2\) by the two forward
coordinates
\[
  \alpha_s=\frac{\rho_s-\rho_{s,c_s-1}}
                  {\rho_{s,c_s}-\rho_{s,c_s-1}},\qquad s=1,2.
\]
The cell-local degree-\(m\) representation is
\[
  M_{c_1,c_2}(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)=
  \sum_{i_1=0}^{m}\sum_{i_2=0}^{m}
  C^{(c_1,c_2)}_{i_1,i_2}
  B_{i_1}^{m}(\alpha_1)B_{i_2}^{m}(\alpha_2).
\]

For \(\ell\) parameters, the Cartesian product of \(\ell\) node vectors
creates
\(\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}(N_s-1)\) hyperrectangular cells.  With
\(\boldsymbol c=(c_1,\ldots,c_\ell)\),
\(\boldsymbol\alpha\in[0,1]^\ell\), and
\(\boldsymbol i=(i_1,\ldots,i_\ell)\),
\[
  M_{\boldsymbol c}(\boldsymbol\alpha)=
  \sum_{\boldsymbol i\in\{0,\ldots,m\}^{\ell}}
  C^{(\boldsymbol c)}_{\boldsymbol i}
  \prod_{s=1}^{\ell}B_{i_s}^{m}(\alpha_s),
  \qquad
  B_i^m(\alpha)=\binom mi(1-\alpha)^{m-i}\alpha^i.
\]
There are \((m+1)^\ell\) coefficients per cell.  Physical-cell subscripts and
local labels use the same Cartesian-product order, with earlier dimensions
varying more slowly.  This convention is visible through \code{cells},
\code{lbls}, and \code{coeffs}.  Cell-local spline and gridding constructions
for parameter-dependent LMIs provide the control-theoretic motivation for this
partitioned architecture \cite{masubuchi1998,xu2024}.

\subsection{Why Bernstein Coordinates}

The monomial and Bernstein bases span the same polynomial space, but the
Bernstein basis matches this package's cell architecture.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Shared-face \(C^0\) ownership.}  Adjacent cells are continuous
        when the complete Bernstein coefficient arrays on their common face
        are the same.  \code{pdvar} enforces this structurally by reusing the
        same YALMIP handles, including edges and corners in several dimensions;
        no continuity equality LMIs are needed.
  \item \textbf{Exact products by convolution.}  Products of basis functions
        have binomial-scaled convolution coefficients.  Matrix multiplication
        therefore stays in the same cell-local representation while preserving
        operand order.
  \item \textbf{Exact degree elevation.}  Coefficients of different degrees
        can be aligned without changing the represented polynomial or adding
        decision freedom.
  \item \textbf{Finite sign evidence.}  The basis is nonnegative and sums to
        one, so sign-definite coefficient matrices certify the matrix sign on
        the whole cell.  Failure of this sufficient test is not proof of
        infeasibility.
\end{itemize}

Bernstein convex-hull bounds have long been used for interval-parameter
robustness \cite{zettler1998}; their relation to broader polynomial and LMI
certificates is surveyed in \cite{chesi2010}.

Appendix~\ref{app:bernstein-math} gives the conversion, elevation, product,
differentiation, and subdivision formulas.  Appendix~\ref{app:certificates}
places direct coefficient tests and the implemented relaxations beside the
standard SOS certificates.

\subsection{From Known Data To Solver Constraints}

The public modeling pipeline separates representation from certification:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Construct known matrix data with \code{pdmat(gridVectors, source)}.
        A source may be a function handle, a global Bernstein coefficient
        grid, or an explicit nested cell-local coefficient tree.  Supplying
        \code{Degree=m} establishes coefficient evidence and validates the
        represented polynomial.
  \item Construct affine decision functions with \code{pdvar}.  Degree zero
        shares one decision matrix across the full grid; degree \(m\ge1\)
        creates a continuous global control lattice whose cell views share
        complete faces.  \code{rhodiff} forms cell-local derivative rows and
        enumerates the requested rate-box vertices.
  \item Assemble a residual using supported known-data and affine algebra.
        Comparisons \code{expr <= rhs} and \code{expr >= rhs} return a
        \code{pdlmi} object that retains the original residual and relation.
  \item Choose the certificate.  Direct coefficient-wise assembly is the
        default.  \code{applyPolya} performs exact degree elevation followed by
        the same coefficient test.  \code{applyFullBoxPreorder} instead builds
        independent cell- and rate-row Gram blocks and exact coefficient
        identities for the local box.
  \item Call \code{toYalmip} and use ordinary YALMIP solver settings and
        \code{optimize}.  Solver selection, diagnostics, and feasibility status
        belong to YALMIP, not to \code{pdlmi}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Installation, Verification, And Lookup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Installation}
\label{sec:installation}

Start \matlab{} in the repository root, or set \code{projectRoot} to the
absolute path of the checked-out repository.  Add the package recursively, then
remove the documentation directory from the executable path.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
projectRoot = "path/to/Differential Parameter-Dependent Linear Matrix Inequality";
addpath(genpath(projectRoot));
rmpath(genpath(fullfile(projectRoot, "doc")));
\end{lstlisting}

For local work from the repository root, this package also resolves from:

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
addpath(pwd)
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Verification}
\label{sec:verification}

Run the current MATLAB test entry point from the repository root:

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
results = tests.run_all();
\end{lstlisting}

The test entry point covers helper utilities, \code{pdbase}, \code{pdmat},
\code{pdvar}, and ordinary \code{pdlmi} behavior.  The YALMIP-backed
\code{pdvar} and \code{pdlmi} tests require YALMIP.  Solver smoke tests
additionally require an SDP solver.  The optional cross-validation material in
\code{sos_validation/}, including its full-box package oracle, is not loaded by
\code{tests.run_all} and is not an ordinary package runtime dependency.

\subsection{Smoke Test}
\label{sec:quick-start}

First confirm that MATLAB resolves the four renamed constructors from this
repository and not from another toolbox:

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
which pdbase -all
which pdmat -all
which pdvar -all
which pdlmi -all
\end{lstlisting}

Then exercise known-data inspection and the solver-facing assembly boundary:

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
A = pdmat({[0 1]}, {1, 3}, Degree=1);
T = bernsteinTable(A, "oneLine");
disp(T)
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpoutput]

    _____________    _______________

        {[1]}        "(1-alpha)*1 + alpha*3"
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
yalmip('clear')
P = pdvar(2, {[0 1]}, "symmetric");
C = P >= 0;
F = toYalmip(C);

\code{F} is the direct coefficient certificate; \code{Fbox} is the default

ordinary YALMIP calls, for example:

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]

\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\code{pdbase}}{pdbase} Constructor}
\label{sec:pdbase-constructor}

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
obj = pdbase(gridVectors, matrixSize, degree)
obj = pdbase(gridVectors, matrixSize, degree, localValues)
obj = pdbase(..., IsContinuous=tf)
obj = pdbase(..., ContainsDecision=tf)
obj = pdbase(..., HasRateDependence=tf)
obj = pdbase(..., RateBounds=rb)
obj = pdbase(..., SourceSummary=txt)
\end{lstlisting}

\arghead
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \code{gridVectors} is a cell vector with one strictly increasing node
        vector per parameter, such as \code{{[0 1 2], [10 20]}}.
  \item \code{matrixSize} is a positive two-element matrix size, such as
        \code{[2 2]}.
  \item \code{degree} is a nonnegative integer Bernstein degree, such as
        \code{1}.
  \item \code{localValues} is the nested physical-cell storage.  When omitted,
        \code{pdbase} creates a zero coefficient-backed object.
  \item \code{IsContinuous}, \code{ContainsDecision}, and
        \code{HasRateDependence} are logical metadata scalars.  They default
        to false; subclasses set them to describe their own payload contract.
  \item \code{RateBounds} is empty or an \(\ell\times2\) matrix of finite lower
        and upper rate bounds, such as \code{RateBounds=[-1 1; -2 2]}.
        Nonempty bounds also mark the object as rate-dependent.
  \item \code{SourceSummary} is inspectable origin text and defaults to
        \code{"coefficient-backed"}.
\end{itemize}

\examplehead

two physical cells because the first parameter has two intervals and the second
parameter has one interval.

\end{lstlisting}

\refhead{Validation and errors}
Malformed grid containers and node vectors raise
\codeerr{pdbase}{InvalidGrid} and
\codeerr{pdbase}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{GridVector}.
Invalid matrix sizes, degrees, or rate bounds raise
\codeerr{pdbase}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{MatrixSize},
\codeerr{pdbase}{InvalidDegree}, and
\codeerr{pdbase}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{RateBounds}.  The nested tree must contain exactly one
leaf per physical cell and \((m+1)^\ell\) coefficient columns per leaf;
violations raise
\codeerr{pdbase}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{LocalValues} or
\codeerr{pdbase}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{CoefficientCell}.  Payload matrices must have the
declared size and be finite, real, and either numeric or supported real YALMIP
expressions.  Malformed payloads raise
\codeerr{pdbase}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{CoefficientPayload}.  Rate-affine payload structures
and \code{HasRateDependence=true} require compatible nonempty
\code{RateBounds}.

The nesting follows physical-cell subscripts, not the Bernstein labels.  Start
with \code{LocalValues\{i1\}}, then take one cell index per remaining

\subsection{Inspection: \texorpdfstring{\code{cells}}{cells}, \texorpdfstring{\code{lbls}}{lbls}, And \texorpdfstring{\code{coeffs}}{coeffs}}

\syntaxhead

\arghead
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \code{obj} is a \code{pdbase} object or a \code{pdmat}/\code{pdvar}
        subclass instance using the shared cell-local storage convention.
  \item \code{cellSubs} selects one physical cell for \code{coeffs}.  Supply a
        numeric row vector such as \code{[2 1]} or a cell array of scalar
        subscripts such as \code{{2,1}}.
  \item \code{cells} returns physical hypercube subscripts in the shared
        traversal order; \code{lbls} returns local Bernstein labels in the
        shared coefficient order.
  \item \code{c} is the flat coefficient cell array for the selected physical
        cell, ordered consistently with \code{lbls(obj)}.
\end{itemize}

\examplehead
The label order is shared by \code{pdmat}, \code{pdvar}, and \code{pdlmi}.  In
this two-parameter degree-one object, each physical cell stores four
coefficient entries with labels \([0,0]\), \([0,1]\), \([1,0]\), and
\([1,1]\).

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
obj = pdbase({[0 1 2], [10 20]}, [1 1], 1);
lbls(obj)
cells(obj)
c = coeffs(obj, [2 1]);
numel(c)
\end{lstlisting}

ans =
     1     1
     2     1

ans =
     4
\end{lstlisting}

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\code{elevVals}}{elevVals}}
\label{sec:pdbase-elevvals}

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
vals = obj.elevVals(degreeIncrement)
\end{lstlisting}

\arghead
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \code{degreeIncrement} is a finite nonnegative integer scalar.  It is
        added to \code{obj.Degree} in every parameter direction; zero returns
        coefficient evidence at the current degree.
\end{itemize}

\refhead{Output}
\code{vals} is a nested \code{LocalValues}-shaped cell tree.  Each physical
cell contains coefficients of degree \code{obj.Degree + degreeIncrement}, with
the represented polynomial, physical-cell tree, and any rate-row ordering
preserved.  The method does not modify \code{obj} or its stored coefficient
evidence.

\refhead{Validation and errors}
An invalid increment raises \code{pdbase:InvalidDegreeIncrement}.  A
function-only \code{pdmat} has no stored Bernstein coefficient evidence, so
this method raises \code{pdbase:MissingCoefficientEvidence}.

\textit{Relationship to backend and public algebra.}  Unlike protected
\code{bernElev}, which elevates one flat cell coefficient family for internal
alignment, \code{elevVals} is a public whole-object query returning elevated
\code{LocalValues}.  Public coefficient algebra uses \code{bernElev}
internally when compatible operands need degree alignment; it does not mutate
either operand.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\code{elevLocalValues}}{elevLocalValues}}
\label{sec:pdbase-elevlocalvalues}

\textit{Internal/protected mechanism.}  This method elevates a temporary
nested coefficient tree after public algebra has remapped operands to a common
physical grid.  It is documented as backend context, not as a supported user
call form.

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
vals = elevLocalValues(obj, vals, fromDeg, toDeg)
vals = elevLocalValues(obj, vals, fromDeg, toDeg, grid)
\end{lstlisting}

Each leaf must be a cell array with one column per degree-\code{fromDeg}
Bernstein label.  The method elevates each rate-vertex row independently to
\code{toDeg}; it never mixes rows.  The optional \code{grid} supplies the
temporary physical-cell layout, and otherwise defaults to
\code{obj.GridInfo.Vectors}.  Equal source and target degrees return the input
tree unchanged.  A malformed leaf raises
\code{pdbase:InvalidCoefficientCell}.  Public users encounter this behavior
through grid merging, assignment, concatenation, and arithmetic rather than by
calling the protected method directly.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\code{bernElev}}{bernElev}}
\label{sec:pdbase-bernelev}

\textit{Internal/protected mechanism.}  This signature documents the backend
algorithm used by public algebra; it is not a supported user call form.

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
out = bernElev(obj, coeffs, fromDeg, toDeg)
\end{lstlisting}

\arghead
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \code{coeffs} is a flat coefficient-cell family in the same label
        order as \code{lbls(obj)}.
  \item \code{fromDeg} and \code{toDeg} are nonnegative integer degrees, with
        \code{toDeg >= fromDeg}.
  \item \code{out} contains \((\code{toDeg}+1)^\ell\) elevated coefficients
        in the same label order.
\end{itemize}

\examplehead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
A = pdmat({[0 1]}, {[1 2]}, Degree=1);
B = pdmat({[0 1]}, {1, 2, 3}, Degree=2);
C = A + B;
C.Degree
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpoutput]
ans =
     2
\end{lstlisting}

The example invokes the protected helper through public addition; users do not
call \code{bernElev} directly.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\code{bernProd}}{bernProd}}
\label{sec:pdbase-bernprod}

\textit{Internal/protected mechanism.}  This signature documents the backend
algorithm used by public matrix multiplication; it is not a supported user
call form.

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
out = bernProd(obj, lhs, lhsDeg, rhs, rhsDeg)
\end{lstlisting}

\arghead
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \code{lhs} and \code{rhs} are flat coefficient-cell families whose
        matrix payload inner dimensions are compatible for multiplication.
  \item \code{lhsDeg} and \code{rhsDeg} are their nonnegative local Bernstein
        degrees.
  \item \code{out} is the binomial-normalized coefficient convolution at
        degree \code{lhsDeg + rhsDeg}; payload products follow MATLAB matrix
        multiplication rules.
\end{itemize}

\examplehead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
A = pdmat({[0 1]}, {[1 2]}, Degree=1);
P = A * A;
P.Degree
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpoutput]
ans =
     2
\end{lstlisting}

The example invokes the protected helper through public matrix multiplication;
users do not call \code{bernProd} directly.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\code{mergeGrid}}{mergeGrid}}
\label{sec:pdbase-mergegrid}

\textit{Internal/protected mechanism.}  This signature documents the backend
grid-refinement algorithm used by public algebra; it is not a supported user
call form.

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
grid = obj.mergeGrid(errId, other1, other2, ...)
\end{lstlisting}

\arghead
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \code{errId} is the error identifier used when parameter counts or
        physical bounds are incompatible.
  \item \code{other1, other2, ...} are coefficient-backed operands, including
        function-backed operands constructed with an explicit \code{Degree}.
        They must have equal parameter counts and matching first and last nodes

        algebra re-expresses operands on these cells before degree elevation,
        coefficient-wise addition, or product convolution.
\end{itemize}
This protected helper is invoked by public algebra methods rather than called
directly by users.

\examplehead

         0    0.5000    1.0000
\end{lstlisting}

The example invokes the protected helper through public addition; users do not
call \code{mergeGrid} directly.

\section{\texorpdfstring{\code{pdmat}}{pdmat} Reference}
\label{sec:pdmat}

\code{pdmat} stores known finite real matrix data on a tensor grid.  Objects
can be coefficient-backed, function-only, or function-backed with explicit
Bernstein coefficient evidence.  Coefficient-backed objects participate in
algebra.  Function-only objects evaluate exactly through the stored handle, but
do not expose coefficient evidence for \code{bernsteinTable} or coefficient algebra.

\subsection{Construction}
\label{sec:pdmat-construction}

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
A = pdmat(gridVector, source)
A = pdmat(gridVector, source, Degree=m)
A = pdmat(gridVectors, source)
A = pdmat(gridVectors, source, Degree=m)
\end{lstlisting}

\arghead
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \code{gridVector} is a numeric vector used as a one-parameter grid
        shorthand, such as \code{[0 1 2]}.
  \item \code{gridVectors} is a cell vector with one numeric grid vector per
        parameter, such as \code{{[0 1], [10 20]}}.
  \item \code{source} may be a function handle, a global cell grid of numeric
        Bernstein coefficients, or explicit nested \code{LocalValues}; for
        example, \code{{1, 2, 3}} is scalar degree-two data on one cell.
  \item \code{Degree=m} sets the local Bernstein degree.  For function handles,
        omitting \code{Degree} creates a function-only object; passing a value
        such as \code{Degree=2} validates and stores Bernstein coefficient
        evidence while retaining the exact function handle for \code{evaluate}.
\end{itemize}

\examplehead
The scalar-grid shorthand is the shortest coefficient-backed form.  The three
numeric cells below are the global Bernstein coefficients for two physical
cells with degree one.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
A = pdmat([0 1 2], {10, 20, 30}, Degree=1)
size(A)
A.Degree
A.SourceSummary
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpoutput]
A =
  pdmat [1 1] over 1-D grid, degree 1, source coefficient-backed

ans =
     1     1

ans =
     1

ans =
    "coefficient-backed"
\end{lstlisting}

For tensor grids, pass one grid vector per parameter.  The source cell array
matches the global coefficient grid implied by the two parameter directions.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
B = pdmat({[0 1], [10 20]}, {1 2; 3 4}, Degree=1)
B.GridInfo.Vectors{2}
B.lbls()
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpoutput]
B =
  pdmat [1 1] over 2-D grid, degree 1, source coefficient-backed

ans =
    10    20

ans =
     0     0
     0     1
     1     0
     1     1
\end{lstlisting}

Use explicit nested \code{LocalValues} when each physical cell must be written
directly.  Here each cell stores two \(1\times2\) row-vector coefficients.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
localVals = {{[1 0], [0 1]}, {[2 0], [0 2]}};
C = pdmat({[0 1 2]}, localVals, Degree=1)
size(C)
C.coeffs(2)
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpoutput]

  pdmat [1 2] over 1-D grid, degree 1, source coefficient-backed

ans =
     1     2

ans =
  1x2 cell array
    {[2 0]}    {[0 2]}
\end{lstlisting}

When \code{source} is a function handle and \code{Degree} is omitted, the
object keeps the exact evaluator but does not claim coefficient evidence.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
F = pdmat({[0 1]}, @(rho) [rho rho^2])
F.SourceSummary
F.evaluate(0.5)
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpoutput]
F =
  pdmat [1 2] over 1-D grid, degree 1, source function

ans =
    "function"

ans =
    0.5000    0.2500
\end{lstlisting}

Add \code{Degree} for a polynomial function when later coefficient inspection
or algebra should use Bernstein evidence.  The exact function handle is still
used for point evaluation.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
G = pdmat({[0 1]}, @(rho) rho.^2, Degree=2)
G.SourceSummary
G.coeffs(1)
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpoutput]
G =
  pdmat [1 1] over 1-D grid, degree 2, source function-bernstein

ans =
    "function-bernstein"

ans =
  1x3 cell array
    {[0]}    {[0]}    {[1]}
\end{lstlisting}

These forms all create finite known-data objects, but only the coefficient-
backed and function-Bernstein forms can participate in coefficient algebra.

\refhead{Public property}
\code{FunctionHandle} retains the supplied function handle for function-only
and function-Bernstein sources; it is empty for cell-backed sources.  Its set
access is private.

\refhead{Validation and errors}
Constructor options must be name--value pairs.  Malformed syntax raises
\codeerr{pdmat}{InvalidOptions}; backend-only options raise
\codeerr{pdmat}{Unsupported}\allowbreak\code{Option}; unknown names raise
\codeerr{pdmat}{Unknown}\allowbreak\code{Option}.  Grid errors use
\codeerr{pdmat}{InvalidGrid} or
\codeerr{pdmat}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{GridVector}.

An unsupported source kind raises \codeerr{pdmat}{InvalidSource}.  Function
arity or initial-call failures raise
\codeerr{pdmat}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{FunctionHandle}; empty, complex, or
otherwise invalid initial outputs raise
\codeerr{pdmat}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{FunctionOutput}.  Global coefficient
grids with inconsistent finite-real payload sizes raise
\codeerr{pdmat}{InvalidData}.  Incompatible inferred or explicit degrees raise
\codeerr{pdmat}{InvalidDegree}; malformed nested cell storage raises
\codeerr{pdmat}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{LocalValues}.  Malformed explicit
coefficient-leaf counts or shapes raise
\codeerr{pdmat}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{CoefficientCell}; invalid, nonfinite,
nonreal, or wrong-size coefficient payloads raise
\codeerr{pdmat}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{CoefficientPayload}.  With an explicit
\code{Degree}, a function that is not representable at that degree raises
\codeerr{pdmat}{NonBernstein}\allowbreak\code{Polynomial}; a failed or
size-inconsistent validation probe raises
\codeerr{pdmat}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{FunctionValue}.  Nested local data
with mismatched shared faces is retained as discontinuous data and emits
warning \codeerr{pdmat}{Discontinuous}\allowbreak\code{LocalValues}.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\code{evaluate}}{evaluate}}
\label{sec:pdmat-evaluate}

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
val = evaluate(A, point)
val = A.evaluate(point)
\end{lstlisting}

\arghead
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \code{A} is a \code{pdmat} object.
  \item \code{point} is a finite real scalar for a one-parameter object, such
        as \code{0.25}, or a finite real row vector with one entry per
        parameter, such as \code{[0.25 12]}.  Every entry must lie within the
        corresponding grid bounds.
  \item \code{val} is a numeric matrix with \code{size(A)}.  Coefficient-backed
        objects use local Bernstein interpolation; function-backed objects call
        their stored function handle.
\end{itemize}

\refhead{Validation and errors}
A point with the wrong type, length, or finite-real shape raises
\codeerr{pdmat}{InvalidPoint}; a point outside any grid bound raises
\codeerr{pdmat}{PointOutOfBounds}.  If a stored function fails or returns a
nonfinite, complex, empty, or size-inconsistent matrix at the requested point,
\code{evaluate} raises
\codeerr{pdmat}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{FunctionValue}.

For a degree-one scalar coefficient-backed object on \([\rho_0,\rho_1]\),
\[
  A(\rho) = (1-\alpha)A_0+\alpha A_1,\qquad
  \alpha = \frac{\rho-\rho_0}{\rho_1-\rho_0}.
\]
The same local-coordinate rule is applied entrywise for matrix-valued data.

\examplehead
Use the function-call form when the object is naturally one input among several
MATLAB expressions.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
A = pdmat({[0 1]}, {2, 4}, Degree=1);
val = evaluate(A, 0.25)
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpoutput]
val =
    2.5000
\end{lstlisting}

At \(\rho=0.25\), the public local coordinate is \(\alpha=0.25\), so the
interpolated value is \(0.75\cdot2+0.25\cdot4=2.5\).

The dot-call form is equivalent and can be convenient in command-window
exploration.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
A = pdmat({[0 1]}, {2, 4}, Degree=1);
val = A.evaluate(0.75)
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpoutput]
val =
    3.5000
\end{lstlisting}

Function-backed objects route through the retained function handle, so
non-polynomial expressions can still be evaluated exactly at a point.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
F = pdmat({[0 pi]}, @(rho) sin(rho));
val = F.evaluate(pi/2)
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpoutput]
val =
     1
\end{lstlisting}

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\code{bernsteinTable}}{bernsteinTable}}
\label{sec:pdmat-table}

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
T = bernsteinTable(A)
T = bernsteinTable(A, cellSubs)
T = bernsteinTable(A, "oneLine")
T = bernsteinTable(A, cellSubs, "oneLine")
\end{lstlisting}

\arghead
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \code{cellSubs} selects one physical cell.
  \item \code{"oneLine"} returns one row per selected physical cell with a
        compact Bernstein expression.
  \item Without \code{"oneLine"}, the full table uses seven metadata columns;
        the output below shows their exact MATLAB names.
\end{itemize}

For one parameter, the \code{Basis} column reports
\[
  \binom{m}{j}(1-\alpha)^{m-j}\alpha^j.
\]
For multiple parameters, the basis is the tensor product of that expression in
each local coordinate, using \code{alpha1}, \code{alpha2}, and so on.
\code{LocalIndex} is the local Bernstein label.
\code{CoeffSubscript} maps the local label back to the global coefficient
subscript on the physical grid.  \code{IsPhysicalNode} is true when that
coefficient lies on a physical grid node.

\examplehead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
A = pdmat({[0 1]}, {[0 1], [1 2]}, Degree=1);
T = bernsteinTable(A, 1, "oneLine");
disp(T)
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpoutput]
    CellSubscript          Expression
    _____________    _______________________

        {[1]}        "(1-alpha)*[0 1] + alpha*[1 2]"
\end{lstlisting}

Use the full table when the metadata columns are the point of the example:

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
A = pdmat({[0 1]}, {1, 2, 3}, Degree=2);
T = bernsteinTable(A);
disp(T)
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpwideoutput]
    TermIndex    CellSubscript    CoeffSubscript    LocalIndex      Basis      IsPhysicalNode    Value
    _________    _____________    ______________    __________    _________    ______________    _____

        1            {[1]}            {[1]}           {[0]}       "(1-alpha)^2"       true          {[1]}
        2            {[1]}            {[2]}           {[1]}       "2(1-alpha)alpha"   false         {[2]}
        3            {[1]}            {[3]}           {[2]}       "alpha^2"           true          {[3]}
\end{lstlisting}

\refhead{Validation and errors}
A function-only object has no coefficient evidence and raises
\codeerr{pdmat}{FunctionOnly}\allowbreak\code{BernsteinTable}.  Unknown text
options, repeated selectors, or invalid physical-cell selectors raise
\codeerr{pdmat}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{BernsteinTableInput}.

\arghead

\subsection{Algebra Operators}
\label{sec:pdmat-algebra}

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
C = A + B
C = A + M
C = M + A
C = A - B
C = A - M
C = M - A
C = +A
C = -A
C = A * B
C = A * M
C = M * A
\end{lstlisting}

\arghead
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Coefficient-backed \code{pdmat}: addition, subtraction, unary signs,
        and multiplication use local Bernstein coefficients.
  \item Numeric scalar, such as \code{5}, broadcasts to the \code{pdmat} matrix
        size when needed.
  \item Numeric matrix: accepted when the matrix size is compatible with the
        operation, such as \code{eye(2)} for a \(2\times2\) object.
  \item Mixed grids with same bounds: re-expressed on a common refinement.
  \item Mixed degrees: elevated to a common degree for coefficient-wise
        operations; multiplication produces the degree sum.
  \item Function-only \code{pdmat}: evaluates and plots, but does not enter
        coefficient algebra unless explicit \code{Degree} supplied coefficient
        evidence.
\end{itemize}

For addition and subtraction, the package elevates both operands to a common
degree before combining matching coefficients:
\[
  C_j = \widehat A_j + \widehat B_j.
\]
For multiplication, coefficient labels convolve and the output degree is the
sum of the input degrees.

\examplehead
Addition elevates the lower-degree operand before combining coefficients.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
A = pdmat({[0 1]}, {1, 2}, Degree=1);
B = pdmat({[0 1]}, {10, 20, 30}, Degree=2);
S = A + B
cS = S.coeffs(1);
[cS{:}]
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpoutput]

  pdmat [1 1] over 1-D grid, degree 2, source coefficient-backed

ans =
   11.0000   21.5000   32.0000
\end{lstlisting}

Operands with the same bounds but different node densities are independently
re-expressed on the union grid before their coefficients are combined.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
A = pdmat({[0 1]}, {1, 2}, Degree=1);
B = pdmat({[0 0.5 1]}, {10, 20, 30}, Degree=1);
S = A + B;

c2 = S.coeffs(2);
fprintf('Merged grid:\n');

fprintf('Degree: %d\n', S.Degree);
fprintf('Cell 1 coefficients:\n');
disp([c1{:}])
fprintf('Cell 2 coefficients:\n');
disp([c2{:}])
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpoutput]

Degree: 1
Cell 1 coefficients:
   11.0000   21.5000

Cell 2 coefficients:
   21.5000   32.0000
\end{lstlisting}

Multiplication raises degree from \(1\) and \(2\) to \(3\).

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]

   10.0000   20.0000   36.6667   60.0000
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]

\subsubsection{Operator-level entries}
\label{sec:pdmat-operator-entries}

\textbf{\code{plus}.} Adds two coefficient-backed objects or adds a compatible
numeric scalar/matrix.  The supported forms are
\code{C=A+B}, \code{C=A+M}, and \code{C=M+A}; grids are refined and degrees
are elevated before coefficient-wise addition.  For example,
\code{size(A+3)} returns the same matrix size as \code{A}.

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
C = A + B
C = A + M
C = M + A
\end{lstlisting}

\textbf{\code{minus}.} Subtracts compatible known-data objects or numeric
operands.  \code{A-M} and \code{M-A} preserve the left/right order of the
matrix subtraction.

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
C = A - B
C = A - M
C = M - A
\end{lstlisting}

\textbf{\code{uplus} and \code{uminus}.} Unary plus returns an equivalent
coefficient-backed object; unary minus negates every local coefficient.

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
B = +A
B = -A
\end{lstlisting}

\textbf{\code{mtimes}.} Performs a matrix product, scalar product, or numeric
matrix product.  The inner matrix dimensions must agree and the output degree
is the sum of operand degrees for two parameter-dependent operands.

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
C = A * B
C = A * M
C = M * A
\end{lstlisting}

\examplehead
For a two-parameter cell, multiplying two degree-one scalar value grids raises
\code{Degree} to 2 and produces \((2+1)^2=9\) tensor coefficients.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
grid = {[0 1], [10 20]};
A = pdmat(grid, {1 2; 3 4}, Degree=1);
B = pdmat(grid, {5 6; 7 8}, Degree=1);

fprintf('Tensor labels:\n');

fprintf('Tensor coefficients:\n');
disp([cK{:}])
\end{lstlisting}

     5     8    12    11    15    20    21    26    32
\end{lstlisting}

\examplehead
Unary signs and numeric addition preserve the implemented \code{pdmat} type
and evaluation behavior.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]

\refhead{Validation and errors}
Incompatible operands for \code{+}, \code{-}, and \code{*} raise
\codeerr{pdmat}{InvalidAddition},
\codeerr{pdmat}{InvalidSubtraction}, or
\codeerr{pdmat}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{Multiplication}.  Parameter counts
or outer bounds that cannot be merged raise
\codeerr{pdmat}{Mixed}\allowbreak\code{Grid}.
Coefficient algebra on a function-only object raises
\codeerr{pdmat}{FunctionOnly}\allowbreak\code{Algebra}.  Exact equality comparisons that cannot
form a compatible comparison grid raise
\codeerr{pdmat}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{Equality}.

\subsection{Matrix Methods}
\label{sec:pdmat-matrix-ops}

Matrix methods act on every local Bernstein matrix coefficient; they do not
rearrange the scheduling grid.  Structural operations require coefficient
evidence.  A function-only \code{pdmat} can still use \code{evaluate},
\code{plot}, \code{uplus}, \code{squeeze}, shape inspection, and equality;
other structural operations reject it with \code{pdmat:FunctionOnlyAlgebra}.
The following families separate matrix shape from the Bernstein grid that
remains attached to the result.

\needspace{7\baselineskip}
\subsubsection{Shape Inspection And Identity}
\label{sec:pdmat-matrix-shape}

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
sz = size(A)
sz = size(A, dim)
n = length(A)
n = height(A)
n = width(A)
n = numel(A)
n = ndims(A)
B = +A
tf = isequal(A, B, ...)
\end{lstlisting}

\arghead
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item All shape queries report the two-dimensional matrix payload, never the
        physical-cell grid.  In particular, \code{ndims(A)} is \code{2}.
  \item Unary \code{+} returns an equivalent \code{pdmat}; it does not alter
        the stored coefficient evidence.
  \item \code{isequal} returns a logical scalar.  Coefficient-backed objects
        are compared after compatible grid refinement and degree elevation;
        function-only objects compare their stored metadata and handle.
\end{itemize}

\needspace{7\baselineskip}
\subsubsection{Transpose, Vectorization, Diagonals, And Reshape}
\label{sec:pdmat-matrix-transform}

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
B = transpose(A)
B = ctranspose(A)
B = A.'
B = A'
B = vec(A)
B = diag(A)
B = diag(A, k)
B = reshape(A, [m n])
B = reshape(A, m, n)
B = squeeze(A)
\end{lstlisting}

For finite real payloads, \code{transpose} and \code{ctranspose} have the same
numerical effect.  \code{vec} uses MATLAB column-major vectorization.
\code{diag} extracts a diagonal, or constructs a square diagonal matrix from a
vector payload; its finite integer \code{k} may not select an empty diagonal.
\code{reshape} accepts exactly two positive dimensions, with at most one
inferred \code{[]} dimension, and preserves the matrix element count.
\code{squeeze} retains the package's two-dimensional matrix convention.

Invalid diagonal offsets or empty diagonal selections raise
\codeerr{pdmat}{InvalidDiag}; malformed or size-changing reshape requests
raise \codeerr{pdmat}{InvalidReshape}.  These transformations require
coefficient evidence and otherwise raise
\codeerr{pdmat}{FunctionOnlyAlgebra}.

\examplehead
These structural methods return new \code{pdmat} objects whose matrix size
changes while the grid and degree metadata remain attached to the coefficient
storage.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
A = pdmat({[0 1]}, {[1 2; 3 4], [10 20; 30 40]}, Degree=1);
V = vec(A)
size(V)
D = diag(A)
size(D)
R = reshape(A, [1 4])
size(R)
\end{lstlisting}

     4     1

  pdmat [2 1] over 1-D grid, degree 1, source coefficient-backed

ans =
     2     1

  pdmat [1 4] over 1-D grid, degree 1, source coefficient-backed

ans =
     1     4
\end{lstlisting}

\subsubsection{Triangular Parts And Reductions}
\label{sec:pdmat-matrix-reductions}

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
B = tril(A)
B = tril(A, k)
B = triu(A)
B = triu(A, k)
B = trace(A)
B = sum(A)
B = sum(A, dim)
B = sum(A, "all")
B = mean(A)
B = mean(A, dim)
B = mean(A, "all")
B = cumsum(A)
B = cumsum(A, dim)
\end{lstlisting}

\code{tril} and \code{triu} retain the lower or upper part of each payload;
\code{k} is a finite integer diagonal offset.  \code{trace} returns a
\(1\)-by-\(1\) \code{pdmat}.  For \code{sum}, \code{mean}, and \code{cumsum},
\code{dim} is a positive integer matrix dimension; \code{"all"} is additionally
accepted by \code{sum} and \code{mean}.

Invalid triangular offsets raise
\codeerr{pdmat}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{TriangularPart}.  Invalid call forms
for \code{sum}, \code{mean}, or \code{cumsum} raise
\codeerr{pdmat}{InvalidSum}, \codeerr{pdmat}{InvalidMean}, or
\codeerr{pdmat}{InvalidCumsum}.  These coefficient transformations also reject
function-only objects with \codeerr{pdmat}{FunctionOnlyAlgebra}.

\examplehead
Summary methods follow the same coefficient-wise rule.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]

\end{lstlisting}

\needspace{7\baselineskip}
\subsubsection{Reordering And Repetition}
\label{sec:pdmat-matrix-reorder}

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
B = flip(A)
B = flip(A, dim)
B = flipud(A)
B = fliplr(A)
B = rot90(A)
B = rot90(A, k)
B = repmat(A, m)
B = repmat(A, m, n)
B = repmat(A, [m n])
\end{lstlisting}

\code{flip} uses MATLAB's first nonsingleton matrix dimension by default;
\code{flipud} and \code{fliplr} select rows and columns.  \code{rot90}
defaults to one quarter-turn and accepts finite integer \code{k}.  \code{repmat}
accepts a positive scalar \code{m}, expanded to \code{[m m]}, two positive
integer repetition counts, or a two-element count vector.  These transformations
leave the physical grid and Bernstein degree unchanged.

Invalid flip dimensions, quarter-turn counts, or repetition shapes raise
\codeerr{pdmat}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{Flip},
\codeerr{pdmat}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{Rot90}, or
\codeerr{pdmat}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{Repmat}.  A function-only object raises
\codeerr{pdmat}{FunctionOnly}\allowbreak\code{Algebra} for coefficient-transforming forms.

\needspace{7\baselineskip}
\subsubsection{Block Assembly}
\label{sec:pdmat-matrix-assembly}

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
C = horzcat(A, B, ...)
C = vertcat(A, B, ...)
C = [A, B]
C = [A; B]
C = cat(dim, A, B, ...)
C = blkdiag(A, B, ...)
\end{lstlisting}

The bracket forms call \code{horzcat} and \code{vertcat}; \code{cat} supports
only dimensions 1 and 2.  Every nonnumeric operand must be coefficient-backed,
and at least one operand of \code{cat} or \code{blkdiag} must be a
\code{pdmat}.  Finite real numeric blocks are promoted on the common grid.
Compatible grids with the same bounds are merged and lower-degree operands are
elevated before their coefficient payloads are assembled.

Incompatible block sizes, operands, or \code{cat} syntax raise
\codeerr{pdmat}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{Concatenation}; dimensions other than
1 or 2 raise \codeerr{pdmat}{Unsupported}\allowbreak\code{CatDimension}.
Invalid \code{blkdiag} inputs raise \codeerr{pdmat}{InvalidBlkdiag}.
Function-only operands raise \codeerr{pdmat}{FunctionOnlyAlgebra}, and
incompatible parameter bounds raise \codeerr{pdmat}{MixedGrid}.

\examplehead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
A = pdmat({[0 1]}, {1, 2}, Degree=1);
size([A, A])
size([A; A])
size(blkdiag(A, 5))
\end{lstlisting}

     1     2

ans =
     2     1

ans =
     2     2
\end{lstlisting}

\subsubsection{Equality And MATLAB Indexing Protocol}
\label{sec:pdmat-matrix-protocol}

\code{isequal(A,B,...)} compares coefficient evidence rather than point
samples.  The \code{end(A,k,n)} and \code{numArgumentsFromSubscript(A,S,ctx)}
methods support ordinary two-index expressions and dot access.  Matrix slicing
and assignment are documented in \cref{sec:pdmat-indexing}; \code{subsref} and
\code{subsasgn} are MATLAB protocol methods, not a second storage API.

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
tf = isequal(A, B, ...)
idx = end(A, k, n)
n = numArgumentsFromSubscript(A, S, ctx)
B = A(1:end, end)
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Indexing And Assignment}
\label{sec:pdmat-indexing}

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
B = A(rows, cols)
value = A.PropertyName
A(rows, cols) = numericValue
A(rows, cols) = B
\end{lstlisting}

\arghead
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \code{rows} and \code{cols} are ordinary MATLAB matrix subscripts,
        such as \code{:}, \code{end}, \code{1}, or \code{2:3}.
  \item \code{PropertyName} is a public property or method name for dot access.
  \item \code{numericValue} is a finite real matrix with the selected block
        size; \code{B} is a compatible coefficient-backed \code{pdmat} block.
  \item \code{value} is the property or method result.  \code{B} is a sliced
        \code{pdmat}; assignment updates every local coefficient and elevates
        degrees when required.
\end{itemize}

\examplehead
Indexing returns a smaller \code{pdmat}; assignment updates the corresponding
matrix entries in every local coefficient.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
A = pdmat({[0 1]}, {[1 2 3; 4 5 6], [10 20 30; 40 50 60]}, Degree=1);
lastCol = A(:, end)
topTail = A(1, 2:3)
A(:, 2) = 5;
A
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpoutput]
lastCol =
  pdmat [2 1] over 1-D grid, degree 1, source coefficient-backed

topTail =
  pdmat [1 2] over 1-D grid, degree 1, source coefficient-backed

A =
  pdmat [2 3] over 1-D grid, degree 1, source coefficient-backed
\end{lstlisting}

The assignment does not change the object degree because the numeric value is
promoted to degree-one coefficient data before insertion.

\refhead{Validation and errors}
Invalid row or column selectors raise \codeerr{pdmat}{InvalidSubscript}.
Linear indexing, deletion, or non-parenthesis assignment raises
\codeerr{pdmat}{Unsupported}\allowbreak\code{Assignment}; an incompatible
right-hand side raises \codeerr{pdmat}{InvalidAssignment}.  Slicing or
assignment on a function-only object raises
\codeerr{pdmat}{FunctionOnlyAlgebra}.

\subsection{Limits}
\label{sec:pdmat-limits}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Function-only objects created without \code{Degree} do not have
        Bernstein coefficient evidence for \code{bernsteinTable} or coefficient algebra.
  \item Numeric operands must be finite real nonempty matrices with compatible
        sizes, or finite real scalars that can broadcast to the required size.
  \item Grid refinement is supported for matching parameter dimensions and
        matching grid bounds.
\end{itemize}

\section{\texorpdfstring{\code{pdvar}}{pdvar} Reference}
\label{sec:pdvar}

\code{pdvar} creates continuous cell-local Bernstein decision expressions backed
by YALMIP \code{sdpvar} coefficients.  It participates in supported affine and
known-data algebra.  It does not choose solvers or call \code{optimize};
solver-facing work happens after comparison overloads create \code{pdlmi}
objects and \code{toYalmip} converts them to YALMIP constraints.

\subsection{Construction}
\label{sec:pdvar-construction}

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
P = pdvar(n, gridVectors)
P = pdvar(n, n, gridVectors)
P = pdvar(n, m, gridVectors)
P = pdvar(..., "full")
P = pdvar(..., "symmetric")
P = pdvar(..., Degree=0)
P = pdvar(..., Degree=1)
P = pdvar(..., Degree=d)
P = pdvar(..., RateBounds=rb)
\end{lstlisting}

\arghead
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \code{pdvar(n, gridVectors)} creates an \(n\times n\) square variable
        using scalar-size shorthand; for example, \code{pdvar(2, {[0 1]})}.
        Square variables default to symmetric YALMIP coefficients.
  \item \code{pdvar(n, n, gridVectors)} is the explicit square-size form; for
        example, \code{pdvar(2, 2, {[0 1]})}.  It has the same default
        symmetric structure as the shorthand.
  \item \code{pdvar(n, m, gridVectors)} creates an \(n\times m\) variable.
        Rectangular variables default to full YALMIP coefficients; for example,
        \code{pdvar(2, 3, {[0 1]})}.
  \item \code{"full"} and \code{"symmetric"} explicitly choose the YALMIP
        coefficient structure.  \code{"symmetric"} requires a square size.
  \item \code{Degree=1} is the default continuous Bernstein decision variable.
        Every finite nonnegative integer \code{Degree=d} is supported.
        \code{Degree=0} creates one parameter-independent decision matrix
        shared across the grid; \code{d >= 1} creates continuous piecewise
        Bernstein data whose neighboring cells share complete control faces.
  \item \code{RateBounds=rb} stores parameter-rate metadata for later
        \code{rhodiff(P)} calls.  Use \code{RateBounds=[-1 1]} for one
        parameter; use a two-row matrix for two parameters, as shown in the
        rate-metadata example below.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\code{rhodiff}}{rhodiff}}
\label{sec:pdvar-rhodiff}

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
D = rhodiff(P)
D = rhodiff(P, rb)
\end{lstlisting}

\arghead
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \code{P} is a \code{pdvar} without existing rate-vertex rows.
  \item \code{rb} is a finite \(\ell\)-by-\(2\) numeric matrix of lower and
        upper parameter-rate bounds, with each lower bound no greater than its
        upper bound.  It must equal \code{P.RateBounds} when the object already
        carries nonempty bounds.
  \item Without \code{rb}, \code{rhodiff(P)} requires nonempty
        \code{P.RateBounds}.
  \item \code{D} is a discontinuous \code{pdvar} with one coefficient row per
        rate vertex.  In one parameter a degree-\(m\) derivative has degree
        \(m-1\), while a degree-zero derivative remains zero; multivariate
        partials are elevated into the implementation's common tensor degree.
\end{itemize}

For a one-parameter degree-one coefficient pair \(P_0,P_1\) on a cell of width
\(h\), the rate-dependent derivative row is
\[
  \dot P(\rho,\dot\rho) = \dot\rho\,\frac{P_1-P_0}{h}.
\]
The implementation stores one row for each allowed rate vertex.

\refhead{Validation and errors}
Unsupported call arity or differentiating an expression that already contains
rate-vertex rows raises \codeerr{pdvar}{InvalidDiff}.  Explicit malformed rate
bounds raise \codeerr{pdvar}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{RateBounds}.  Calling
\code{rhodiff(P)} without stored bounds raises
\codeerr{pdvar}{Missing}\allowbreak\code{RateBounds}; explicit bounds that
disagree with \code{P.RateBounds} raise
\codeerr{pdvar}{RateBounds}\allowbreak\code{Mismatch}.

\examplehead
When rate bounds live on the object, \code{rhodiff(P)} uses them directly.

\subsection{Matrix Methods}
\label{sec:pdvar-matrix-ops}

Matrix methods act on each local YALMIP-backed payload while preserving
compatible grid, degree, and rate metadata.  This is matrix structure, not
parameter-grid manipulation.  The families below use the same organization as
the \code{pdmat} reference, but their outputs remain \code{pdvar} expressions
and supported assembly may additionally use coefficient-backed \code{pdmat},
affine \code{sdpvar}, and numeric blocks.

\label{sec:pdvar-matrix-shape}

\syntaxhead

\subsection{Limits}
\label{sec:pdvar-limits}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Constructor-created \code{pdvar} objects support every finite
        nonnegative integer degree.  Higher degree increases the number of
        Bernstein control matrices per cell.
  \item Products may contain decision variables on at most one side; nonlinear
        decision products such as \code{P * Q} are outside this slice.
  \item Function-only \code{pdmat} operands cannot enter \code{pdvar} algebra
        unless they were constructed with explicit Bernstein evidence.
  \item \code{rhodiff} of an existing rate-vertex derivative expression is not
        part of the current public workflow.
\end{itemize}

\section{\texorpdfstring{\code{pdlmi}}{pdlmi} Reference}

and ordinary YALMIP solver calls.

C = pdlmi(expr, relation, UsePolya=true, PolyaDegree=d)
C = pdlmi(expr, relation, "UsePolya", "PolyaDegree", d)
C = pdlmi(expr, relation, "UseFullBoxPreorder")
C = pdlmi(expr, relation, UseFullBoxPreorder=true)
C = pdlmi(expr, relation, FullBoxOrder=r)
C = pdlmi(expr, relation, UseFullBoxPreorder=true, FullBoxOrder=r)
C = pdlmi(expr, relation, UseFullBoxPreorder=false, FullBoxOrder=r)
C = lhs <= rhs
C = lhs >= rhs
\end{lstlisting}

\arghead
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \code{expr} is a square \code{pdvar} expression whose coefficient
        matrices are symmetric or Hermitian; \code{relation} is exactly
        \code{"<="} or \code{">="}.
  \item \code{UsePolya} is a logical scalar option with default
        \code{false}. Its bare form, \code{"UsePolya"}, and
        \code{UsePolya=true} without a degree select increment one.
  \item \code{PolyaDegree=d} is a finite nonnegative integer scalar. It
        elevates the residual by \code{d} in every parameter direction before
        assembling constraints. Supplying it without \code{UsePolya} enables
        P\'olya and issues \codeerr{pdlmi}{ImplicitUsePolya};
        \code{UsePolya=false} with positive \code{d} raises
        \codeerr{pdlmi}{ConflictingPolyaOptions}.
  \item \code{UseFullBoxPreorder} is a logical scalar option with default
        \code{false}.  Its bare form and the explicit value \code{true} select
        full-box assembly at the smallest admissible order.
  \item \code{FullBoxOrder=r} is a finite nonnegative integer scalar and is an
        absolute order, not a degree increment.  Supplying it without
        \code{UseFullBoxPreorder} enables full-box assembly.  When full-box
        assembly is enabled, the minimum is \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) for a
        one-parameter residual of degree \(m\), and \(\lceil m/2\rceil\) for
        two or more parameters.  Explicitly pairing
        \code{UseFullBoxPreorder=false} with \code{FullBoxOrder=r} suppresses
        that automatic selection: \code{C} remains direct and records \code{r}
        only as inspectable metadata; the order is not applied to assembly.
  \item P\'olya and full-box selection are mutually exclusive.  Enabling both
        raises \codeerr{pdlmi}{Conflicting}\allowbreak\code{Relaxations}.
  \item \code{C} is direct coefficient-wise assembly unless one opt-in mode is
        selected. Comparisons such as \code{lhs >= rhs} are the ordinary
        user-facing entry point and accept compatible numeric, \code{pdmat},
        affine \code{sdpvar}, and \code{pdvar} right-hand sides.
\end{itemize}

The inspectable properties \code{UsePolya}, \code{PolyaDegree},
\code{UseFullBoxPreorder}, and \code{FullBoxOrder} report the selected mode and
order metadata.  Their set access is private.  Comparison-created direct
objects, and direct constructor calls that omit \code{FullBoxOrder}, have both
selectors false and both numeric properties zero.  The explicit-false
combination described above is the exception: it retains \code{FullBoxOrder=r}
while \code{UseFullBoxPreorder} remains false and the stored constraints remain
direct.

The constructor rejects a non-\code{pdvar} residual with

\subsection{Direct Coefficient Assembly}
\label{sec:pdlmi-direct}

For each physical cell, local Bernstein coefficient, and rate-vertex row if
present, \code{pdlmi} creates one YALMIP semidefinite constraint.  For an
ordinary expression with \(N_c\) physical cells and \(N_b\) Bernstein
coefficients per cell, the number of stored constraints is \(N_cN_b\).  For a
\code{rhodiff} expression with \(N_v\) rate vertices, the count becomes
\(N_cN_bN_v\).

\subsection{Opt-In P\'olya Assembly And \texorpdfstring{\code{applyPolya}}{applyPolya}}
\label{sec:pdlmi-polya}

With \code{UsePolya=true}, \code{pdlmi} first elevates the stored residual by
\code{PolyaDegree} in every parameter direction, then constrains every
elevated local coefficient and every active rate row. Thus, if the original
degree is \(m\), the count is
\(N_c (m+d+1)^\ell\) for ordinary expressions and
\(N_c (m+d+1)^\ell N_v\) for rate-vertex expressions. An increment of zero is
valid and has the same coefficient count as direct assembly.

\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
Cpolya = C.applyPolya()
Cpolya = C.applyPolya(d)
\end{lstlisting}

\code{applyPolya} is a value-class method: it returns a new \code{pdlmi},
leaves \code{C} unchanged, and rebuilds from \code{C.Residual}. Selecting a
new increment therefore replaces rather than compounds a previous selection.
The no-argument form uses one; an invalid increment raises
\code{pdlmi:InvalidPolyaDegree}.

\examplehead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]

\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Full Box Bernstein--Gram Preordering And
\texorpdfstring{\code{applyFullBoxPreorder}}{applyFullBoxPreorder}}
\label{sec:pdlmi-full-box}

The full-box mode is a cell-local positive-semidefinite representation in the
forward local coordinates \(\alpha_s\in[0,1]\).  It is a box-specific Bernstein--Gram
preordering, not a general Putinar certificate, general-domain SOS interface,
or full-space SOS representation.  It adds no implicit positivity margin.

\needspace{10\baselineskip}
\syntaxhead
\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpsyntax]
Cbox = C.applyFullBoxPreorder()
Cbox = C.applyFullBoxPreorder(r)
\end{lstlisting}

The no-argument form selects the smallest admissible absolute order.  The
explicit form selects \code{r}; it does not add \code{r} to the residual
degree.  Both forms are value-class selectors: they return a new \code{pdlmi},
leave \code{C} unchanged, and rebuild from \code{C.Residual}.  Reapplication
therefore replaces an earlier full-box order or a P\'olya selection rather than
compounding it.

For a one-parameter residual of degree \(m\), the minimum and matching target
degree are parity-specific:
\[
  r_{\min}=\left\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\right\rfloor,
  \qquad
  m_{\mathrm{target}}=
  \begin{cases}
    2r, & m\text{ even},\\
    2r+1, & m\text{ odd}.
  \end{cases}
\]
For an \(n\)-by-\(n\) residual, define the lifted, binomially normalized basis
in forward index order by
\[
  \mathcal Z_q(\alpha)=
  \begin{bmatrix}
    B_0^q(\alpha)I_n & \cdots & B_q^q(\alpha)I_n
  \end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The positive-semidefinite target has the even-degree form
\[
  \mathcal Z_r(\alpha)^{\mathsf T}Q_0\mathcal Z_r(\alpha)
  +\alpha(1-\alpha)\mathcal Z_{r-1}(\alpha)^{\mathsf T}
    Q_1\mathcal Z_{r-1}(\alpha),
\]
where the second block is omitted at order zero.  For odd degree it has the
form
\[
  (1-\alpha)\mathcal Z_r(\alpha)^{\mathsf T}Q_0\mathcal Z_r(\alpha)
  +\alpha\mathcal Z_r(\alpha)^{\mathsf T}Q_1\mathcal Z_r(\alpha).
\]
Every present Gram matrix \(Q_i\) is constrained positive semidefinite.

For \(\ell>1\) parameters, the minimum is
\(r_{\min}=\lceil m/2\rceil\), and the target has tensor degree \(2r\) in
every direction.  For a tensor multi-degree \(\boldsymbol d\),
\(\mathcal Z_{\boldsymbol d}(\boldsymbol\alpha)\) denotes the tensor product of
the normalized one-dimensional bases above, lifted by \(I_n\) in the same
forward label order.  One term is included for every mask
\(\boldsymbol\mu\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}\):
\[
  \sum_{\boldsymbol\mu\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}}
  \left(\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}
    [\alpha_s(1-\alpha_s)]^{\mu_s}\right)
  \mathcal Z_{r\boldsymbol 1-\boldsymbol\mu}(\boldsymbol\alpha)^{\mathsf T}
  Q_{\boldsymbol\mu}
  \mathcal Z_{r\boldsymbol 1-\boldsymbol\mu}(\boldsymbol\alpha).
\]
A term whose tensor Gram degree has a negative component is omitted.  For an
\(n\)-by-\(n\) residual, a present mask has Gram dimension
\(n\prod_s(r-\mu_s+1)\).

Assembly is independent for every physical cell and every active rate-vertex
row; Gram variables are not shared across either boundary.  For \code{>=}, the
method represents the residual.  For \code{<=}, it negates the target before
forming the positive-semidefinite representation.  Within each cell and row,
the stored constraints are ordered as all PSD Gram blocks followed by exact
equalities matching every Bernstein coefficient.  Consequently, the number of
stored constraints is not the direct coefficient count.

\refhead{Validation and errors}
Malformed orders raise \codeerr{pdlmi}{InvalidFullBoxOrder}; an admissible
integer below the minimum raises \codeerr{pdlmi}{FullBoxOrderTooLow}.  The
constructor rejects malformed selectors with
\codeerr{pdlmi}{InvalidUse}\allowbreak\code{FullBox}\allowbreak\code{Preorder},
and simultaneous
P\'olya and full-box selection raises\newline
\codeerr{pdlmi}{Conflicting}\allowbreak\code{Relaxations}.  The usual
expression, relation, option, square-matrix, and symmetry checks described in
\cref{sec:pdlmi-construction} apply unchanged.

\section{Cross-Class Helper Reference}
\label{sec:helpers}

The \code{+helper} namespace contains exactly nine implementation utilities
whose ownership genuinely crosses class boundaries.  They are developer-facing
building blocks, not an alternative modeling API: ordinary users should enter
through \code{pdbase}, \code{pdmat}, \code{pdvar}, and \code{pdlmi}.  Their
signatures and current consumers are listed below so maintainers can distinguish
shared contracts from class-local implementation details.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0.65em]
  \item \code{helper.bernTbl(...)}\\
        Builds ordinary, rate-vertex, or one-line Bernstein inspection tables
        with caller-supplied value and expression formatters.
        \textit{Consumers:} \code{pdmat}, \code{pdvar}.
  \item \code{helper.cellGet(vals, subs)}\\
        Follows one tensor-subscript row through nested \code{LocalValues} and
        returns the selected leaf.  \textit{Consumers:} all four classes.
  \item \code{helper.chk(val, id, msg, ...)}\\
        Applies shared predicate tags and bounds, returning the input or
        raising the caller's error ID.  \textit{Consumers:} all four classes.
  \item \code{helper.combRows(vecs)}\\
        Returns Cartesian-product rows with earlier dimensions varying more
        slowly.  \textit{Consumers:} all four classes.
  \item \code{helper.isZero(val, mode, ...)}\\
        Classifies numeric, additive, nested-payload, or coefficient-backed
        object zero evidence.  \textit{Consumers:} \code{pdmat}, \code{pdvar}.
  \item \code{helper.mapVals(vals, fcn, grid)}\\
        Maps one function over every coefficient in a nested cell tree.
        \textit{Consumers:} \code{pdmat}, \code{pdvar}.
  \item \code{helper.matSubs(subs, sz, id)}\\
        Normalizes numeric, logical, or colon matrix subscripts into row and
        column indices.  \textit{Consumers:} \code{pdmat}, \code{pdvar}.
  \item \code{helper.mkGrid(grid, owner)}\\
        Validates tensor-grid vectors and builds \code{GridInfo} with vectors,
        points, bounds, and node counts.  \textit{Consumers:} \code{pdbase},
        \code{pdmat}, \code{pdvar}.
  \item \code{helper.mkNest(counts, fcn[, prefix])}\\
        Constructs recursive tensor storage and calls the leaf factory with
        each complete subscript row.  \textit{Consumers:} \code{pdbase},
        \code{pdmat}, \code{pdvar}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Shared Validation And Errors}

\code{helper.chk} recognizes the predicate tags \code{numeric}, \code{real},
\code{cell}, \code{struct}, \code{nonempty}, \code{scalar}, \code{vector},
\code{matrix}, \code{finite}, \code{integer}, \code{positive},
\code{nonnegative}, \code{increasing}, and \code{rowbounds}; its named tests are
\code{Size}, \code{Numel}, \code{MinNumel}, \code{Min}, and \code{Max}.
Failed user-data checks raise the supplied class error ID.  An unknown tag or a
missing validator-option value raises
\codeerr{helper}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{ValidatorCall}.

\code{helper.isZero} accepts modes \code{"num"}, \code{"add"},
\code{"vals"}, and \code{"obj"}.  Unknown modes raise
\codeerr{helper}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{ZeroMode}.  A wrong number of
mode-specific inputs raises
\codeerr{helper}{Invalid}\allowbreak\code{ZeroCall}.
\code{helper.matSubs} and
\code{helper.bernTbl} deliberately receive a caller-owned error ID, so public
indexing and table errors remain in the owning class namespace.

\subsection{Class-Private Helpers}

Single-class mechanics remain beside their owner rather than in
\code{+helper}.  The private area of \code{pdbase} owns coefficient-tree
validation.  The private area of \code{pdmat} owns source fitting, continuity,
grid conversion, matrix scanning, and algebraic wrapping.  The private area of
\code{pdvar} owns affine and rate-row packing, product routing, value-tree
zipping, and symbolic wrapping.  The private area of \code{pdlmi} owns direct
and full-box assembly, Gram conversion, and order validation.
These functions have no supported direct call form.  Their placement is an
ownership boundary: a helper should move into a class when only that class can
meaningfully use it, and should enter \code{+helper} only after a real
cross-class contract exists.

\subsection{Parameter-Dependent Lyapunov Solver Smoke}
\label{sec:parameter-dependent-lyapunov-smoke}

The first solver smoke test in \code{+tests/+pdlmi/test_solver_smoke.m}
contrasts a constant Lyapunov matrix with a degree-one, parameter-dependent
one.  It solves the same decay and positivity constraints in both cases.  The
degree-one solution is then materialized as known \code{pdmat} data and
plotted.  \code{pdvar} is a decision-expression class and does not itself
provide \code{plot}; applying \code{value} to its local YALMIP payloads after
a successful solve supplies the numeric Bernstein coefficients for
\code{pdmat}.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
yalmip('clear')
grid = {[0 1]};
A = pdmat(grid, @(x) (1 - x) * [-1 -1; 1 -1] + ...
    x * [-1 -10; 0.1 -1], Degree=1);
solver = 'lmilab';
if exist('mosekopt', 'file') ~= 0
    solver = 'mosek';
end
opts = sdpsettings('solver', solver, 'verbose', 0);

Pc = pdvar(2, grid, Degree=0);
CconstantDecay = Pc * A + A' * Pc <= -1e-10 * eye(2);
CconstantPositive = Pc >= eye(2);
solConstant = optimize([CconstantDecay.toYalmip, ...
    CconstantPositive.toYalmip], [], opts);

Pd = pdvar(2, grid);
CdependentDecay = Pd * A + A' * Pd <= -1e-10 * eye(2);
CdependentPositive = Pd >= eye(2);
solDependent = optimize([CdependentDecay.toYalmip, ...
    CdependentPositive.toYalmip], [], opts);
assert(solDependent.problem == 0, solDependent.info)

Pplot = pdmat(grid, ...
    {cellfun(@value, Pd.LocalValues{1}, UniformOutput=false)}, ...
    Degree=Pd.Degree);
figure(Name="Parameter-dependent Lyapunov matrix")
plot(Pplot, SamplesPerCell=40, LineWidth=1.5)
title("Solved parameter-dependent Lyapunov matrix")
\end{lstlisting}

The figure contains one curve for each entry of the solved \(2\)-by-\(2\)
matrix \(P(\rho)\), sampled on the parameter interval.  It is a diagnostic
visualization of the feasible solution, not a certificate beyond the
coefficient-wise constraints supplied to the solver.  The constant-case result
is retained to exercise the comparison in the smoke test; only a MOSEK solve
is used there to certify its intended infeasibility distinction.

\subsection{Rate-Dependent Block LMI Solver Smoke}
\label{sec:solver-example}

This example is adapted from \code{+tests/+pdlmi/test_solver_smoke.m}.  The
goal is to find a parameter-dependent \(P(\rho)\) and scalar \(\gamma\) such
that, on every Bernstein coefficient and rate vertex,
\[
\begin{bmatrix}
\dot P + PA + A^\top P & PB & C^\top\\
B^\top P & -\gamma I & D^\top\\
C & D & -\gamma I
\end{bmatrix}
\preceq 0,
\qquad
P(\rho)\succeq 0.
\]
The derivative term \(\dot P\) is built with \code{rhodiff(P, [-1 1])}.  The
comparison operators create \code{pdlmi} wrappers, and \code{toYalmip} hands
the coefficient-wise constraints to YALMIP.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=dpmatlab]
yalmip('clear')
grid = linspace(0, 1, 2);
A = pdmat(grid, @(x) [-1, 0.5; -1, -2] + ...
    x * [-1.3, -20; 2, -10], Degree=1);
B = pdmat(grid, @(x) [1, -4; -1, -1] + ...
    x * [2.2, 0.5; -6, -5], Degree=1);
Cmat = eye(2);
Dmat = zeros(2);

P = pdvar(2, grid);
diffP = rhodiff(P, [-1 1]);
gamma = pdvar(1, grid, Degree=0);
E1 = [diffP + P * A + A' * P, P * B, Cmat';
      B' * P, -gamma * eye(2), Dmat';
      Cmat, Dmat, -gamma * eye(2)] <= 0;
E2 = P >= 0;
\end{lstlisting}

\begin{lstlisting}[
  style=dpmatlab,
  title={\textcolor{dpblue}{\sffamily\bfseries\footnotesize MATLAB input (continued)}}
]
objective = gamma.LocalValues{1}{1};
solver = 'lmilab';
if exist('mosekopt', 'file') ~= 0
    solver = 'mosek';
end
opts = sdpsettings('solver', solver, 'verbose', 0);
sol = optimize([E1.toYalmip, E2.toYalmip], objective, opts);
assert(sol.problem == 0, sol.info)
gammaValue = value(objective);
assert(isfinite(gammaValue))
fprintf("Optimal H-infinity gamma: %.6g\n", gammaValue)

numel(E1.Constraints)
numel(E2.Constraints)
\end{lstlisting}

The \code{fprintf} call prints the actual numeric \(\gamma\), whose value is
solver- and tolerance-dependent; the final two commands report six and two
constraints.

\appendix

\section{Bernstein Basis Mathematics}
\label{app:bernstein-math}

This appendix collects the representation identities used throughout the
package.  They are exact algebraic identities, not approximation steps.  See
Farouki's retrospective for historical and numerical background
\cite{farouki2012}.

\subsection{Forward Coordinates And Tensor Basis}

On the physical interval \([\rho^-,\rho^+]\), define
\[
  \alpha=\frac{\rho-\rho^-}{\rho^+-\rho^-}\in[0,1],
  \qquad
  B_i^m(\alpha)=\binom mi(1-\alpha)^{m-i}\alpha^i.
\]
The endpoint identities are
\(B_0^m(0)=1\) and \(B_m^m(1)=1\).  Every basis function is nonnegative on the
interval and \(\sum_i B_i^m=1\).  For
\(\boldsymbol\alpha\in[0,1]^\ell\), the tensor basis is
\[
  B_{\boldsymbol i}^{m}(\boldsymbol\alpha)
  =\prod_{s=1}^{\ell}B_{i_s}^{m}(\alpha_s).
\]
This manual uses the same degree in every coordinate because that is the
current storage contract; the identities extend componentwise to mixed degree.

\subsection{Exact Monomial Conversion}

Let \(p(\alpha)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}a_j\alpha^j
=\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_k B_k^n(\alpha)\).  Power coefficients convert to Bernstein
coefficients by
\[
  b_k=\sum_{j=0}^{k}
  \frac{\binom{k}{j}}{\binom{n}{j}}a_j,
  \qquad k=0,\ldots,n.
\]
The inverse conversion is
\[
  a_j=\binom{n}{j}\sum_{i=0}^{j}
  (-1)^{j-i}\binom{j}{i}b_i,
  \qquad j=0,\ldots,n.
\]
For matrices these formulas act entrywise; for tensor polynomials they act
along one axis at a time.  Basis conversion keeps degree fixed.  It is
different from degree elevation.

\subsection{Lossless Degree Elevation}

One elevation step from degree \(m\) to \(m+1\) gives
\[
  \widehat C_k=
  \frac{k}{m+1}C_{k-1}
  +\left(1-\frac{k}{m+1}\right)C_k,
\]
with absent endpoint terms interpreted as zero.  Direct elevation from degree
\(m\) to \(M\ge m\) is
\[
  \widehat C_k=
  \sum_{i=\max(0,k-M+m)}^{\min(m,k)}
  C_i\frac{\binom mi\binom{M-m}{k-i}}{\binom Mk},
  \qquad k=0,\ldots,M.
\]
Apply the same map successively along each tensor axis.  The polynomial is
unchanged, and the elevated coefficients are determined convex combinations
of the original ones.  Elevation therefore adds no decision variables; a new
higher-degree \code{pdvar} parameterization does.

\subsection{Products As Weighted Convolution}

The basis-product identity
\[
  B_i^m B_j^n=
  \frac{\binom mi\binom nj}{\binom{m+n}{i+j}}B_{i+j}^{m+n}
\]
gives, for ordered matrix factors,
\[
  C_k=\sum_{i+j=k}A_iB_j
  \frac{\binom mi\binom nj}{\binom{m+n}{k}}.
\]
In \(\ell\) dimensions,
\[
  C_{\boldsymbol k}=
  \sum_{\boldsymbol i+\boldsymbol j=\boldsymbol k}
  A_{\boldsymbol i}B_{\boldsymbol j}
  \prod_{s=1}^{\ell}
  \frac{\binom m{i_s}\binom n{j_s}}
       {\binom{m+n}{k_s}}.
\]
Equivalently, multiply each value grid by its tensor binomial weights, perform
ordinary discrete convolution, and divide by the output weights.  Matrix
factor order must not be exchanged.

\subsection{Differentiation}

For cell width \(h=\rho^+-\rho^-\),
\[
  \frac{dp}{d\rho}
  =\frac{m}{h}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}(C_{i+1}-C_i)B_i^{m-1}(\alpha).
\]
For a tensor polynomial, differentiation with respect to \(\rho_s\) takes
forward differences along tensor axis \(s\), scales by \(m/h_s\), and leaves
the other axes unchanged.  Terms in
\(\dot P=\sum_s(\partial P/\partial\rho_s)\dot\rho_s\) may then be elevated to
a common tensor degree.  \code{rhodiff} evaluates this affine rate expression
at the vertices of the supplied rate box and keeps derivative boundary rows
cell-local.

\subsection{de Casteljau Evaluation And Subdivision}

Starting with \(C_i^{(0)}=C_i\), de Casteljau recursion is
\[
  C_i^{(r)}=(1-\alpha)C_i^{(r-1)}+\alpha C_{i+1}^{(r-1)},
  \qquad r=1,\ldots,m.
\]
The value is \(C_0^{(m)}\).  The left and right edge chains of the triangular
recursion are exactly the Bernstein coefficients on the two subintervals cut
at \(\alpha\).  Applying subdivision along each tensor axis gives exact
coefficients on child boxes.  This explains why physical grid refinement can
tighten convex-hull evidence while leaving the polynomial unchanged.  The
current package has no public de Casteljau or adaptive-refinement method.

\section{Polynomial Matrix Sign Certificates On A Hypercube}
\label{app:certificates}

For a residual \(F\preceq0\), write \(S=-F\); for \(F\succeq0\), write
\(S=F\).  The certificate problem is \(S(\boldsymbol\alpha)\succeq0\) on
\([0,1]^\ell\).  Scalar SOS notation below extends to polynomial matrices by
using matrix-SOS Gram representations, or equivalently by requiring the
scalar polynomial \(y^{\mathsf T}S(\boldsymbol\alpha)y\) to have the structured
certificate.  PSD Gram matrices certify non-strict inequalities.  Any desired
strict margin must first be placed in \(S\).

\subsection{Full-Space SOS}

The simplest Gram certificate ignores the box and proves global
nonnegativity:
\[
  p(z)=v_d(z)^{\mathsf T}Qv_d(z),\qquad Q\succeq0,
\]
where \(v_d\) contains monomials through the selected degree.  Matching
coefficients is linear in \(Q\).  This is an SOS certificate on all of
\(\mathbb R^\ell\), so it can be much stronger than positivity only on a box.
It is sufficient, but multivariate nonnegative polynomials need not be SOS.
The package does not expose a full-space SOS constructor.

\subsection{Direct Bernstein Coefficients}

If
\[
  S(\boldsymbol\alpha)=
  \sum_{\boldsymbol i}B_{\boldsymbol i}^{m}(\boldsymbol\alpha)
  C_{\boldsymbol i},
  \qquad C_{\boldsymbol i}\succeq0\quad\text{for all }\boldsymbol i,
\]
then \(S\succeq0\) on the box because the basis matrices form a convex
combination.  This is the package default after residual and rate-vertex
assembly.  It introduces no Gram matrices.  Its converse is false: an
indefinite Bernstein coefficient defeats this certificate, not the underlying
continuous inequality.

\subsection{P\'olya-Type Coefficient Relaxation}

P\'olya's theorem concerns a homogeneous polynomial strictly positive on a
simplex: after multiplication by a sufficiently high power of the sum of the
simplex coordinates, every coefficient becomes positive.  Quantitative bounds
are available under strict positivity hypotheses \cite{powersreznick2001}.
On each box coordinate, the pair \((1-\alpha_s,\alpha_s)\) lies on a
one-simplex and its sum is one.  Multiplication by
\(\prod_s((1-\alpha_s)+\alpha_s)^r=1\) is therefore exact tensor degree
elevation.  It can turn a successful strict certificate into visible positive
Bernstein coefficients without changing the residual.

\code{applyPolya(r)} implements one user-selected elevation increment followed
by the direct coefficient test.  It does not search for a sufficient exponent,
add a margin, or add SOS multipliers.  Failure at a fixed increment is
inconclusive.

\subsection{Putinar Quadratic Modules}

For a basic closed set
\(K=\{z:g_j(z)\ge0,\ j=1,\ldots,q\}\), the quadratic module generated by the
constraints contains polynomials of the form
\[
  \sigma_0(z)+\sum_{j=1}^{q}\sigma_j(z)g_j(z),
  \qquad \sigma_j\ \text{SOS}.
\]
Putinar's Positivstellensatz states that if this module is Archimedean, then
every polynomial strictly positive on \(K\) has such a representation
\cite{putinar1993}.  The Archimedean hypothesis is substantive; compactness
alone is not the statement of Putinar's theorem.  Degree truncation produces a
hierarchy of finite SDPs, and failure at one order is not a nonnegativity
counterexample.  General Putinar modules are mathematical background here,
not a current \code{pdlmi} option.

\subsection{Schm\"udgen And Full Preorderings}

The preordering generated by \(g_1,\ldots,g_q\) also includes every square-free
product:
\[
  p(z)=\sum_{\nu\in\{0,1\}^{q}}
  \sigma_{\nu}(z)\prod_{j=1}^{q}g_j(z)^{\nu_j},
  \qquad \sigma_\nu\ \text{SOS}.
\]
Schm\"udgen's Positivstellensatz gives this representation for polynomials
strictly positive on a compact basic closed semialgebraic set
\cite{schmudgen1991}.  Unlike Putinar's result, its standard hypothesis is
compactness, but the preordering can require many multiplier blocks.

For the hypercube, the package uses the \(\ell\) generators
\(h_s=\alpha_s(1-\alpha_s)\).  Their nonnegative set is exactly
\([0,1]^\ell\), and all \(2^\ell\) subset products form the implemented
box-specific full preordering.  At a selected absolute order,
\code{applyFullBoxPreorder} creates independent PSD Gram blocks for every
physical cell and active rate row, then matches the resulting Bernstein
coefficients exactly.  It is not a general-domain Schm\"udgen interface.

\subsection{The One-Dimensional Markov--Luk\'acs Forms}

In one variable the interval certificate has exact parity-specific forms.  For
degree at most \(2d\),
\[
  p(\alpha)=v_d(\alpha)^{\mathsf T}Q_0v_d(\alpha)
  +\alpha(1-\alpha)
   v_{d-1}(\alpha)^{\mathsf T}Q_1v_{d-1}(\alpha),
  \qquad Q_0,Q_1\succeq0.
\]
For degree at most \(2d+1\),
\[
  p(\alpha)=(1-\alpha)
  v_d(\alpha)^{\mathsf T}Q_0v_d(\alpha)
  +\alpha v_d(\alpha)^{\mathsf T}Q_1v_d(\alpha),
  \qquad Q_0,Q_1\succeq0.
\]
These forms characterize interval nonnegativity at the stated parity, rather
than merely supplying one hierarchy level.  \code{pdlmi} uses their
Bernstein-basis matrix-Gram counterpart when \(\ell=1\) \cite{lukacs1918}.

\subsection{Relationships And Implementation Boundary}

The methods should not be ordered as if they were interchangeable syntax.
Full-space SOS proves a global statement.  Direct Bernstein and P\'olya
coefficient tests exploit convex-hull evidence.  Putinar uses a quadratic
module and requires an Archimedean hypothesis for its strict-positivity
theorem.  Schm\"udgen uses a full preordering and compactness.  The
one-dimensional Markov--Luk\'acs form is exact.  Increasing decision degree,
elevating a fixed residual, refining the physical grid, and increasing Gram
order are four different operations.

The callable package boundary is limited to direct coefficient assembly,
fixed-increment P\'olya elevation, and fixed-order full box preordering.
General full-space SOS, Putinar modules, general-domain preorderings,
adaptive order or subdivision, residual diagnostics, and automatic strictness
margins are not implemented public APIs.  The isolated validation programs
under \code{sos_validation/} compare formulations; they are not runtime
dependencies and do not expand the package interface.
